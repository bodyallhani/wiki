Excessive Load on the Neck: A Biomechanical Analysis of Angles

As the head tilts forward, the load the cervical spine must bear increases exponentially. As the forward tilt angle increases due to forward head posture syndrome, the cervical spine bears a load several times greater than usual, which can lead to structural damage to the disc.

Compression of the cervical segments is closely linked to the manifestation of neurological symptoms; to alleviate nerve root compression caused by cervical disc herniation, an approach that secures physical space between the cervical segments acts as a key mechanism for resolving compressive stimuli on the nerve root. In this context, a biomechanical conservative treatment alternative that can be safely selected prior to surgical intervention is whole-body correction that straightens the back and restores the normal alignment angle of the neck.

Chronic Shoulder Muscle Tension and the Principle of Leverage

The muscle stiffness in the shoulder area commonly reported by patients with forward head posture is attributed to the body's physical defense mechanism that occurs as the neck and thoracic spine become rounded.

  • The Problem of a Fixed Fulcrum: When the rounded back remains fixed, the muscles around the neck must continuously endure an abnormal force.
  • A Fundamental Approach: Beyond simply relaxing localized muscles, correcting the rounded thoracic spine to reset the fulcrum of the lever is necessary to resolve excessive muscle tension.

The Correlation Between Spinal Imbalance and Musculoskeletal Pain

When the skeletal structure is misaligned, the surrounding supportive muscles maintain a state of hypertension 24 hours a day to prevent further displacement of the bones. Muscles and ligaments surrounding a twisted spine are placed under continuous tension to support it, leading to chronic neck pain.

  • Pain Due to Compensatory Mechanisms: Pain in the back of the neck and shoulders signifies that these muscles are being overused to support a twisted skeletal structure.
  • Therapeutic Conclusion: For fundamental symptom relief, it is necessary not only to treat the tense muscles themselves but also to correct the alignment of the lower spine and pelvis to create a structural environment in which the muscles can naturally relax.
