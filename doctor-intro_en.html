Bodyall Medical Team in Suwon: What They Check Matters More Than a Title

Who this page is for

This guide is for patients in Suwon with recurring neck, back or joint pain, arm or leg symptoms, limited movement, or questions about herbal or weight care who want to understand the clinicians’ assessment and treatment-selection process before booking.

Bodyall first checks urgent or neurological risks and whether hospital testing is needed, then records the symptoms and the function that matters most. For musculoskeletal care, the team separates the roles of Chuna, SART, acupuncture, pharmacopuncture and exercise; ultrasound and MET may be used when the examination calls for them. Herbal and weight-care visits separately review medicines, health status, eating and activity records.

How Bodyall turns the examination into a planThe page does not rely on a clinician’s title or equipment count alone. It connects risk screening → target-function recording → treatment-role choice → retesting the same function → the next treatment, exercise or referral decision.

A shared clinical pathway used by the Bodyall team

1. Define risk and diagnostic scope

New or progressive weakness, bowel or bladder change, saddle sensory loss, worsening gait, major trauma, fever or persistent night pain calls for urgent or hospital assessment before Chuna.

2. Record symptoms and function

The clinician records not only pain location but also the function the patient wants to regain—such as sitting, walking, turning the neck, raising an arm, sleeping or eating.

3. Choose treatment roles

The examination guides the roles of myofascial or joint-mobilisation Chuna, SART with Insanggi pelvic support and selected corrective instruments, acupuncture, pharmacopuncture, exercise or herbal medicine. SART and herbal medicine are not used for every patient.

4. Retest the same measure

The same movement and symptom range recorded before treatment is checked again. If there is no useful response or neurological findings progress, the plan changes and imaging, neurological or orthopaedic assessment may take priority.

Frequently asked questions

How much correction-care experience does Bodyall have?

Bodyall has treated 40,000 cumulative correction-care patients through December 2025 (clinic count). The clinic uses this experience to select treatment roles around each patient's symptoms and functional limits, then reassesses the same function.

Which clinician should I book?

Share your symptoms, preferences and available times so the clinic can guide you to an appropriate scope. The key is not a name alone, but whether risk screening, treatment-role explanation and same-function retesting are connected in the actual visit.

When should hospital or urgent assessment come first?

New or progressive weakness, bowel or bladder change, saddle sensory loss, worsening gait, major trauma, fever, persistent night pain, chest pain or breathing difficulty requires urgent or hospital assessment first.

What to prepare before booking

Bring the symptom timeline, your most limited movement, imaging or test results, current medicines and prior treatment history. When booking, state the consultation area and any warning signs so the assessment scope can be planned.
