Suwon Lumbar Disc and Spinal Stenosis: Bodyall Gait and Movement Assessment

Do walking or sitting bring on low-back and leg symptoms?

Bodyall Korean Medicine Clinic in Ingye-dong, Suwon first assesses neurological risk and lumbar, pelvic and hip movement when back-to-leg pain, numbness or walking limits recur. Myofascial Chuna or MET may suit muscle-related restriction; corrective Chuna or SART is selected when joint movement also needs attention and there are no contraindications.

When appropriate, an Insanggi device supports the pelvis during sacroiliac and spinal joint mobilisation. Examination findings guide the site, direction, intensity and need for correction tools. Changes that require urgent assessment

Seek urgent medical assessment for new urinary retention or loss of bladder or bowel control, saddle numbness, rapidly progressive leg weakness or sudden inability to walk. Severe back pain with fever, major trauma, or new pain with a cancer history also needs prompt assessment.[3][4][5]

1. Can pelvic torsion alone explain disc herniation or stenosis?

No. Lumbar disc herniation involves disc tissue protruding through the outer annulus and may irritate a nerve root. Stenosis describes narrowing around neural structures from degenerative changes in bone, joints or ligaments. Age, trauma and repeated loading can contribute, so Bodyall does not determine the cause from pelvic shape or posture alone.[3][4]

  • Load and movement: identifying whether bending, lifting, twisting, sitting, standing or walking aggravates symptoms helps guide daily movement.
  • Diagnosis: posture and palpation are parts of the examination; disc or nerve involvement requires symptom, neurological and, when needed, imaging correlation.

2. What movement and daily limitations does Bodyall examine?

Bodyall begins with symptoms and history, strength and sensation, spinal and pelvic palpation, active and passive range of motion, gait and posture. One or two of the most limited tasks—prolonged sitting, standing, walking, bending or extension—are selected and compared before and after care.

  • Myofascial Chuna and MET: relaxation methods are selected when muscle-fascial tension and pain limit movement. MET involves the patient’s active muscle contraction and relaxation, adapted to examination findings and pain response.
  • Ultrasound and further testing: Bodyall provides ultrasound and explains which area, if any, needs assessment after examination. Leg numbness and walking limits require neurological, joint and, when indicated, vascular differentiation; suspected neurological deficits take priority for imaging or specialist assessment.
  • Corrective Chuna and SART: when appropriate, Insanggi pelvic-support positioning is combined with sacroiliac and spinal joint mobilisation, manual correction and selected tools. Site, direction and intensity follow the examination and the patient’s response.
  • Reassessment: walking distance, sitting or standing time and the original difficult task are checked under the same conditions. Immediate comfort is distinguished from sustained functional change.

3. Lumbar disc herniation and spinal stenosis are not the same

Interpretation limitNot all low-back pain is disc-related.Posture-dependent symptoms alone do not confirm stenosis.
Treatment decisionSeverity, neurological findings, functional limitation and course determine conservative care and the need for specialist assessment.

These are common patterns with exceptions. Imaging and specialist review are used when needed; not every episode of low-back pain requires immediate MRI.[3][4][5]

4. How are SART, acupotomy and bee-venom pharmacopuncture selected?

A 2023 pilot randomized trial assigned 34 patients with lumbar spinal stenosis to two groups and compared eight weeks of acupuncture and interferential-current treatment with or without additional acupotomy. Both groups improved on some pain and function measures, without a clear between-group difference in key analyses. Bodyall uses this as context for judging acupotomy alongside prior treatment response, current walking limits and individual risk—not as a reason to apply it routinely.[1]

Bodyall's actual SART selection process

Neurological risk and fracture risk are checked first. When suitable, pelvic-support positioning, sacroiliac and spinal joint mobilisation, manual correction and selected medical tools are combined. Site, direction and intensity follow the most limited task, which is reassessed after care.

Manual care is selected to manage current pain and movement, without assuming disc restoration or spinal-canal enlargement. Temporary pain or soreness may follow; worsening numbness or weakness requires prompt reassessment. Exercise, daily-movement guidance and necessary specialist care remain part of planning.

Acupotomy suitability and risk

Acupotomy uses a bladed needle and is invasive; pain, bleeding, infection and injury to nerves, blood vessels or surrounding tissues can occur. Bodyall evaluates the site and depth, medicines, bleeding risk and infection before discussing whether it is needed. Whether to combine it with SART depends on current symptoms, the response to previous care and the site that needs treatment.

A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis brought together clinical studies of acupotomy for lumbar spinal stenosis.[2] At Bodyall, the review informs questions for assessment; diagnosis, treatment site, bleeding and infection risk still determine whether acupotomy is appropriate.

Bee-venom allergy-risk consultation

Local and systemic hypersensitivity, including anaphylaxis, can occur. A previous uneventful injection or skin test does not guarantee safety; suitability is judged separately.

Persistent bleeding, fever, swelling, or new sensory or strength changes after acupotomy need prompt care. Shortness of breath, throat swelling or faintness after bee-venom treatment requires emergency help.[6]

5. When should exercise, medication, injection or surgery be discussed?

Without emergency warning signs, appropriate activity adjustment, exercise, medication and rehabilitation can be considered. Prolonged complete rest is usually not the goal. Medicines need to be selected with gastrointestinal, kidney and cardiovascular risks and other medicines in mind.[3][5]

NICE recommends considering manual therapy only as part of a package including exercise for low-back pain and sciatica. It does not recommend traction or acupuncture for this indication, and its general manual-therapy recommendation is not direct evidence for SART, acupotomy or bee-venom pharmacopuncture.[5]

Severe sciatica may require medical consultation about injections. Walking limitation from stenosis and general sciatica are different. Persistent disability or neurological deficit requires correlation with imaging and specialist assessment; necessary medical or surgical evaluation should not be delayed because correction care is underway.[4][5]

6. Preparing for a Bodyall visit and reassessment

Chief Director Donghae Lee is listed by the Society of Korean Medicine as a public-relations director of the Society of Spinal Diagnosis and Correction.[7] Bring prior imaging reports if available and disclose medicines, injections, surgery, allergies and osteoporosis. Do not stop anticoagulants or antiplatelet medicines without consulting the prescriber.

After treatment, the clinician compares walking distance, sitting or standing tolerance and the original difficult movement, not only pain. New or worsening symptoms prompt reassessment before repeating the same treatment.

Q. Do lumbar disc herniation and spinal stenosis always require surgery?

No. Conservative management may be considered according to symptoms and neurological findings. New bowel or bladder dysfunction, saddle numbness or rapidly progressive weakness requires urgent assessment; persistent disability may require a specialist surgical opinion.

Q. Do I have to receive SART, acupotomy and bee-venom pharmacopuncture together?

No. Bodyall first decides whether myofascial Chuna or SART fits the current limitation, while the need and risks of acupotomy and bee-venom pharmacopuncture are assessed separately. The initially difficult walking, sitting or standing task is checked again after the selected treatment.
