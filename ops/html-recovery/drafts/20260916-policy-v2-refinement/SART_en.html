Spinal correction and Chuna in Suwon: How Bodyall SART works

Who may consider a Bodyall SART consultation?

A patient in Suwon with recurring neck or low-back pain, arm or leg symptoms, and a clear limitation such as sitting, walking, turning the head, or raising an arm may discuss whether Chuna or SART fits after examination.

Bodyall considers muscle tension together with movement of the spine and pelvic joints when choosing a correction approach. When SART is appropriate, the clinician uses Insanggi pelvic support while working on the sacroiliac joints and individual spinal segments, adding corrective instruments where needed. The patient then repeats the sitting, walking or head-turning task that was uncomfortable, helping guide the next treatment and advice for daily movement.

1. What is separated before SART?

Mechanical pain and neurological symptoms

Local pain that changes with position or movement is separated from tingling, sensory loss, and weakness. An imaging label alone does not select the treatment.

Adjacent joints such as the hip or shoulder

Pain felt in the back or neck may arise from the hip, sacroiliac joint, shoulder, or peripheral nerve, so related movements are checked.

Seek hospital or emergency assessment first for: new or progressive arm or leg weakness, bladder or bowel change, saddle numbness, worsening gait, major trauma, fever, unexplained weight loss or persistent night pain, chest pain, or shortness of breath.

2. What Bodyall records in the examination

  • Onset, pain distribution, tingling, sensation, and strength changes
  • The most limited task among sitting, standing, walking, turning the head, and raising an arm
  • Active motion of the spine, pelvis, hip, and shoulder plus neurological findings
  • Conditions that require avoiding or modifying Insanggi support, mobilisation, or selected instruments
  • A same-function baseline for comparison after care

Bodyall reports 40,000 cumulative correction-care patients through December 2025, based on the clinic's own count. This correction-care experience informs treatment planning around each patient's examination findings and the movements they find difficult.

3. How the roles of Chuna and SART are separated

Soft-tissue and joint-mobilisation Chuna

This addresses surrounding muscle tension and restricted movement. Soft-tissue and mobilisation techniques are selected after examination, alongside advice on exercise and daily activities.

Insanggi pelvic support within SART

Supporting the pelvis in a lying position helps establish a position for sacroiliac and spinal joint mobilisation. Manual techniques and corrective instruments are selected according to the patient's position and symptom response.

Mobilisation and selected corrective instruments

The clinician chooses the sacroiliac or spinal segment to treat and the intensity based on movement restriction, neurological findings and contraindications. Manual correction and instruments are used where appropriate, with pain and movement responses checked during care.

Exercise, load management, and referral

Activity limits, graded exercise, and repeated load are adjusted. Progressive neurological findings or no change in the same function prompts reconsideration of imaging or neurological/orthopaedic assessment.

4. Retest the same function and decide the next action

What is checked again after care?

The relevant item among sitting time, walking distance, trunk rotation, neck rotation, arm elevation, and the area of tingling is repeated in the same way. No change or a different symptom pattern triggers reassessment rather than automatic repetition.

When booking, note the most limited task, any tingling or weakness, recent imaging, and current medicines.

5. Frequently asked questions

What does the name SART mean?

SART stands for Spine Alignment Restoration Therapy, Bodyall's biomechanical space-decompression Chuna protocol. It aims to improve everyday movements made difficult by pain.

What should I prepare for my appointment?

Tell the clinician which movement is most difficult, when symptoms began, and whether tingling or weakness has changed. Bring any recent imaging and a list of current medicines to help with the examination.

6. Research that helps inform treatment

  1. NICE NG59 advises against traction for low-back pain with or without sciatica and says manual therapy should be considered only as part of a package that includes exercise. Bodyall likewise plans examination-guided manual care together with exercise and advice on daily movement. NICE NG59
  2. Choi et al.'s 30-person study compared a spinal decompression device with general traction over four weeks. Pain, disability and straight-leg-raise measures improved in both groups, with no statistically significant between-group difference. The study examined those device treatments, a different protocol from Bodyall SART. PMID 25729196

Bodyall considers the pain and function measures used in research alongside each patient's examination findings and changes in daily activities when planning subsequent care.
