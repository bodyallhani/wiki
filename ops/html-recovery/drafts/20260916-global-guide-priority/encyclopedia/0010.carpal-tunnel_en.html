Numb thumb, index and middle fingers at night: is it the wrist or a neck nerve?

If hand numbness wakes you at night or you keep dropping objects in Suwon

The distribution of numb fingers, sensation, thumb opposition and thenar strength, wrist provocation and neck, elbow and forearm movement should be checked before assuming carpal tunnel syndrome. At Bodyall Korean Medicine Clinic, Chief Director Donghae Lee hears the patient’s questions directly and distinguishes median nerve compression at the wrist from cervical radiculopathy, proximal median neuropathy, ulnar neuropathy and systemic neuropathy. Wrist protection and load review, acupuncture or pharmacopuncture, myofascial Chuna, corrective Chuna/conditional SART and acupotomy are assigned separate purposes, followed by retesting the same sensation, thumb strength and wrist or neck task.

Which symptoms and findings are assessed together?

Numbness in the thumb, index, middle and thumb-side half of the ring finger that worsens at night or briefly improves by shaking the hand is common in carpal tunnel syndrome. No single wrist provocation test establishes the diagnosis. A 2022 meta-analysis found Phalen, Tinel and Durkan tests useful for screening, but not as stand-alone diagnostic tests.[1] Symptom distribution, sensation, thumb opposition and thenar strength and a cluster of tests are considered together. Electrodiagnostic testing, ultrasound or specialist assessment can be connected when findings are equivocal or another neurological condition is suspected.[2]

The neck and shoulder are tested for relevance, not presumed to be the cause

Carpal tunnel syndrome is compression of the median nerve at the wrist. Coexisting neck pain or whole-arm tingling does not by itself make cervical alignment the cause. Neck movement, neurological distribution and shoulder, elbow and forearm restrictions are checked separately to distinguish wrist from proximal nerve problems.

How does Bodyall distinguish wrist from cervical nerve findings?

Bodyall Korean Medicine Clinic has cared for 40,000 cumulative correction-care patients through December 2025 (clinic count). The chief director compares wrist findings with the neurological findings of the neck and arm in one visit, then selects care for the identified region and purpose. What does the evidence actually support?

The 2024 AAOS guideline summary supports history and examination-based CTS-6 diagnosis, with electrodiagnostic testing or ultrasound considered for equivocal presentations or suspected alternate neurological conditions.[2] A 2025 multicenter retrospective study combined ultrasound-guided acupotomy with corticosteroid injection, so its results concern the combined intervention and do not isolate acupotomy’s effect.[3] In a 2012 randomized trial, acupuncture was not superior to placebo acupuncture when both groups used night splints.[4] Bodyall combines this evidence with examination findings, risks and alternatives to separate the roles of night-splint guidance, acupuncture, pharmacopuncture, Chuna and conditional acupotomy.

Signs that need medical assessment first

Visible thenar wasting, progressive loss of thumb opposition, persistent or rapidly worsening sensory loss, major swelling, discoloration or severe pain after trauma, and fever, a wound or infection signs need prompt assessment. Sudden one-sided arm paralysis with slurred speech or facial droop, or chest pain with shortness of breath, takes priority over wrist care.

Preparing for your first visit

Note which fingers go numb, when it starts, how often it wakes you, whether shaking the hand changes it, what you drop and whether neck movement affects it. Bring prior electrodiagnostic, ultrasound or imaging results and a medication list; mention diabetes, thyroid disease and pregnancy.

For example: “My right thumb, index and middle fingers go numb every night, and I recently dropped a cup.” Include the affected fingers, night symptoms, weakness and effect of neck movement when choosing a time through Bodyall Korean Medicine Clinic booking in Suwon.

Key points

  • No single wrist provocation test establishes carpal tunnel syndrome.
  • Median-nerve distribution, sensation and thumb strength are distinguished from neck and other nerve problems.
  • Chuna and SART are scoped only when neck movement is relevant.
  • The same function is retested; progressive weakness or persistent sensory loss prompts testing and specialist review.
