💡 Q. Does everyone regain the same amount of weight after stopping a GLP-1 medicine?

No. Trial extensions and meta-analyses have found average weight regain after treatment ends, but individual changes vary with the medicine, initial weight loss, food intake, activity, health, and follow-up period. Make a maintenance plan with the prescriber rather than changing the dose or stopping on your own.

1. Distinguish GLP-1 medicines from phentermine

GLP-1 receptor agonists act on a pathway involved in satiety, gastric emptying, and glucose regulation. Phentermine is a different appetite suppressant that acts on the sympathetic nervous system. Their mechanisms, approved uses, duration, contraindications, adverse effects, and discontinuation plans differ. Evidence about stopping a GLP-1 medicine therefore should not be applied directly to phentermine or described as one shared “central nervous system blockade.”

Appetite and energy expenditure may adapt during weight loss. After treatment ends, recording changes in appetite, food intake, activity, and symptoms alongside weight gives the chief director concrete information for the maintenance consultation.

Plan dose changes with the prescriber

Do not reduce, stop, restart, or replace a prescription medicine with another medicine or herbal product on your own. Persistent severe abdominal pain, repeated vomiting or dehydration, fainting or altered consciousness, suspected low blood glucose while using diabetes medicines, and pregnancy or breastfeeding plans require the prescriber or appropriate medical assessment before a routine weight consultation.

2. Why prepare the maintenance plan before treatment ends

A US electronic health record cohort included 125,474 adults with overweight or obesity. Estimated discontinuation within one year was 46.5% among people with type 2 diabetes and 64.8% among those without it. Each 1% weight increase after discontinuation was associated with a 2.3% or 2.8% higher reinitiation hazard, respectively. Because this was observational, weight gain cannot be treated as the only cause of restarting treatment[1].

A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis pooled eight randomized trials with 2,372 participants. Mean regain after discontinuation was 2.20 kg for liraglutide and 9.69 kg for semaglutide or tirzepatide, with a tendency to track the original amount lost. Because these are averages across different medicines and follow-up periods, the consultation also reviews the individual medicine, amount lost, and course after discontinuation[2].

In the STEP 1 extension, participants who had received semaglutide 2.4 mg plus lifestyle intervention for 68 weeks regained about two-thirds of their prior weight loss during the following year after treatment stopped. The semaglutide group’s mean weight change increased by 11.6 percentage points from week 68 to week 120. The extension included only a subset of the original trial and did not continue a separate structured lifestyle intervention after withdrawal[3].

Terms in plain language

Weight regain means weight rises again after loss and varies greatly between people. A cohort study observes patterns and associations in real-world records but usually cannot establish cause and effect. A meta-analysis pools averages across studies; it does not predict an individual outcome.

5. Prepare for a BodyallFit consultation and check suitability

  • List the name, dose, and dates of every injection, prescription, herbal product, and supplement used or stopped.
  • Record weight under similar conditions and waist circumference, if available, along with appetite and gastrointestinal symptoms for two to four weeks.
  • Note changes in food, alcohol, sleep, and activity before and after medication use.
  • Ask about the prescriber’s plan, feasible goals, visit frequency, cost, communication scope, and response or safety stopping criteria.
  • During follow-up, review behaviors, symptoms, adverse effects, and any needed tests as well as weight.
When BodyallFit may be a relevant consultation option

It may be relevant for a patient who wants the chief director to review appetite and lifestyle changes after medication, concurrent products, and health together, then decide whether herbal medicine is suitable and agree on an actionable maintenance plan. When warning signs or medication adverse effects are suspected, contact the prescriber or obtain appropriate medical assessment first.

💡 Q. Do phentermine and GLP-1 medicines work in the same way?

No. GLP-1 receptor agonists and phentermine, a sympathomimetic medicine, differ in mechanism, approved use, risks, and discontinuation planning. Evidence about stopping one medicine should not be transferred directly to the other; discuss each medicine with its prescriber.

💡 Q. How does BodyallFit combine herbal and lifestyle consultation after a weight-management medicine ends?

The chief director reviews medicines used or stopped, appetite and other symptoms, lifestyle records, concurrent products, and health conditions to decide whether herbal medicine is suitable. During prescribed herbal care, patients can ask the same chief director directly about medicine use, food, and exercise, and the plan is adjusted according to changes and adverse effects. Decisions to stop or restart the original medicine remain with its prescriber.

Bring the names, doses, and dates of medicines, injections, herbal products, and supplements used or stopped; recent weight and waist records; appetite and gastrointestinal symptoms; food, sleep, and activity records; and the prescriber’s plan.
