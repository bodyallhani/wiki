Suwon Acupotomy Treatment: Principles, Evidence, Patient Fit and Safety

What to know before deciding on acupotomy
What is it?Acupotomy is an invasive Korean medicine procedure using a flat-tipped instrument to apply mechanical stimulation or attempt release at a soft-tissue site selected after examination.
Who may consider it?It is considered individually when symptoms, examination findings, alternative diagnoses and the response to prior care point to a specific soft-tissue target.
Why consider Bodyall?Bodyall first reproduces the painful movement, checks palpation, range of motion and relevant neurologic findings, then decides separately whether acupotomy, another treatment or SART is appropriate. The same symptom or movement is reassessed after treatment.
What are the cautions?Because the instrument penetrates the skin, pain, bleeding, bruising, infection and injury to nearby nerves, vessels or other tissue are possible. The evidence varies by condition.
Long-lasting pain alone does not confirm an adhesion or an indication for acupotomy.

The same painful area may involve a joint, tendon, nerve, bone or inflammatory disorder. Bodyall reviews the painful movement, sensory or strength changes, previous treatment and test results, and prioritizes other testing or referral when needed.

Who this pathway fits: someone in Suwon who wants an in-person examination to distinguish whether an invasive soft-tissue procedure is appropriate, what role spinal or pelvic correction may have, and how the response will be checked rather than assuming every chronic pain problem needs acupotomy.

How is acupotomy explained?

A flat-tipped acupotomy instrument is inserted through the skin to apply mechanical stimulation or attempt release at a soft-tissue site selected by examination. The 2023 lumbar spinal stenosis study was a pragmatic pilot randomized trial, so it is preliminary evidence[1]. The 2013 clinical study combined acupotomy with other Korean medicine treatments, so it cannot isolate the effect of acupotomy alone[2]. These studies do not establish Bodyall's own outcomes or the effect of combining acupotomy with SART.

What Bodyall checks before acupotomy

The cause of pain and urgent warning signs come before the procedure.
  • Symptoms and function: location, duration, provoking and easing movements, night pain, sensory change and weakness.
  • Differential assessment: whether fracture, infection, inflammatory disease or progressive neurologic loss requires other testing or care first.
  • Bleeding and infection risk: anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs, bleeding disorders, wounds or infection, allergies and previous adverse reactions.
  • Alternatives and goals: rest, exercise, medication, injection, rehabilitation or surgical assessment, together with realistic goals and evidence limits.

Why consider Bodyall before rushing into acupotomy

Bodyall's clinical approach: it does not label every long-standing pain as an adhesion. Bodyall checks symptom reproduction, palpation, active and passive range of motion, relevant neurologic findings and the response to previous care, then decides independently whether acupotomy or another treatment is needed. When a spinal or pelvic problem is identified, SART is selected for its own indication rather than treated as a mandatory step before or after acupotomy. If symptoms do not respond or worsen, the diagnosis and plan are reassessed instead of automatically repeating the procedure.

  • Examination first: posture analysis alone does not determine the diagnosis or procedure site.
  • Acupotomy considered separately: not every chronic pain or imaging abnormality calls for it; no procedure and referral are valid options.
  • SART only when relevant: posture, balance, palpation, range of motion, gait and the main complaint guide the correction direction. This does not mean SART is required around acupotomy or proven to improve its effect.

If the procedure is selected, confirm the target, expected benefit, evidence limits, alternatives, possible adverse effects and aftercare for your individual condition. A lack of response or worsening should trigger reassessment.

Acupotomy FAQ

How is acupotomy different from ordinary acupuncture?

Ordinary acupuncture uses a thin needle. Acupotomy uses a flat-tipped instrument to apply mechanical stimulation or attempt release at a selected soft-tissue target, so examination and a discussion of procedural risk come first.

Does long-lasting pain mean I need acupotomy?

No. Chronic pain is not always caused by adhesion. Symptoms, examination findings, response to prior care and alternative diagnoses should guide the decision.

Is acupotomy safe?

It penetrates the skin, so pain, bleeding, bruising, infection and injury to nearby nerves, vessels or other tissue are possible. Report medicines, bleeding disorders and infection beforehand. Seek prompt assessment for worsening swelling, redness, warmth, fever, bleeding that does not stop, or new or progressive numbness or weakness.
