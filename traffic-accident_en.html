Neck and back pain after a traffic accident: care and insurance in Suwon

What should I do if pain starts after an accident?

After an accident, neck or back pain needs assessment to decide whether further tests or emergency care are necessary. Whiplash is a neck injury caused by sudden head movement. Symptoms can include a painful or stiff neck, headache, and shoulder or arm discomfort, sometimes starting hours later.[3]

At Bodyall Korean Medicine Clinic in Ingye-dong, Suwon, consultation considers your health history, activities that have become difficult, and movement of your neck, back and limbs. Whether SART, acupuncture or herbal medicine is appropriate depends on your examination and current condition.

These symptoms need prompt assessment rather than a routine appointment.

New arm or leg weakness or reduced sensation, difficulty walking, or an electric-shock feeling in the neck or back may require assessment for nerve injury.[3] Following a head injury, reduced consciousness, a seizure, or problems speaking or keeping your balance require emergency help: call 119 in Korea. Seek prompt medical advice for vomiting or dizziness, or if you injured your head while taking a medicine that reduces blood clotting. Do not drive yourself or forcefully turn your neck in these circumstances.[4]

Back pain after a serious accident, symptoms in both legs, reduced feeling around the genitals or anus, or new difficulty urinating or controlling your bladder or bowels require emergency assessment. Ask for help or call 119 instead of driving yourself.[8]

1. Does delayed pain or a normal scan mean my spine is misaligned?

Delayed pain alone does not establish subtle spinal misalignment or tissue adhesions. Assessment should consider symptoms before the accident, the impact, where pain occurs and how it changes, strength, sensation and movement. This illustration is a conceptual explanation, not a diagnostic record or proof of an individual treatment outcome.

Imaging may help identify fractures or other injuries, while whiplash itself may not be visible on a scan. This does not mean that all pain is caused by alignment changes. X-rays, CT or MRI and further medical assessment may be considered when indicated. Not everyone needs the same tests, and ultrasound alone cannot identify every possible injury after an accident.[5]

2. How are treatment and a return to daily activities planned?

The goals are pain management and recovery of movement and daily function. Depending on the injury, options may include medical treatment such as pain medicine, exercise instruction, physiotherapy or specialist assessment. After acute injuries have been assessed, follow your clinician’s advice about gradually resuming permitted activities. Avoid prolonged neck immobility, but follow any collar prescription for an injury that requires one.[3][5]

The assessment and reassessment process at Bodyall

SART (Spine Alignment Restoration Therapy) considers symptoms, health history, palpation (examination by touch), active movement (movement you make yourself), passive movement (movement guided by the clinician), and posture. When suitable, it combines an approach intended to create space and adjust alignment with active-movement reassessment and guidance on daily activities. Suspected fracture, instability or nerve injury after an accident takes priority for assessment and referral. Describing this process does not guarantee restoration of spinal spacing or prevention of persistent symptoms.

The 2022 acupotomy report cited here concerns combined treatment of one 14-year-old patient with nerve entrapment after knee ligament reconstruction. It does not directly establish effectiveness for neck or back pain after a traffic accident, or outcomes at Bodyall.[1] The 2025 review concerns acupotomy plus nerve block for cervical radiculopathy, symptoms involving a nerve root in the neck. Its public abstract reports a difference in the “effective rate”, but no statistically significant between-group difference in the pain scale, with high risk of bias and limited adverse-event reporting. These findings should not be extended to prevention of accident-related symptoms or SART effectiveness.[2]

3. What should I check before using auto insurance?

  • If you plan to use auto insurance, prepare the insurer’s name, accident claim number and claim handler’s contact details.
  • Ask the clinic and insurer about payment authorization, covered treatments and periods, possible patient payments, and required documents.
  • Share previous tests and treatments and your current medicines so the care plan and possible duplication can be reviewed.

A claim number alone does not determine coverage, amounts or the treatment period you need. Check these matters before your visit. Emergency symptoms take priority over paperwork.

5. Questions about care after a traffic accident

💡 Q. Can pain start after I initially felt fine?

Whiplash symptoms can start hours after an accident. Delayed pain alone does not establish spinal misalignment or adhesions. Assessment considers the accident, pain location, movement, strength and sensation.

💡 Q. Do normal test results mean I need no further care?

Pain can persist despite no clear abnormality on a test. Examination of new symptoms and limits on daily activities helps determine further tests and treatment. New limb weakness, altered sensation, walking difficulties or reduced consciousness after a head injury need prompt assessment.

💡 Q. Must I receive SART, herbal medicine, bee venom and acupotomy together?

There is no mandatory combination. Discuss your condition, test results, medicines and allergies, and the expected benefits and risks. Choose appropriate care and adjust the plan according to your response.

💡 Q. Does an auto insurance claim number mean I will pay nothing?

A claim number alone cannot confirm coverage or patient payments for every treatment. Ask your insurer and clinic about payment authorization, covered items and periods, required documents and possible patient payments.

How to interpret the research: A postoperative case involving another body region[1] and combined treatment for cervical radiculopathy[2] are not direct evidence for accident-related symptoms.
