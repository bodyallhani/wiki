Morning Finger Stiffness: Separating Joint Swelling, Trigger Finger, and Nerve Symptoms

When Bodyall may be a useful Suwon option

A single catching or locking finger, warm swelling across several small joints, and numbness or weakness with neck or arm symptoms can all feel like “morning stiffness,” but they need different checks.

Bodyall Korean Medicine Clinic does not begin by assigning one procedure. The clinician observes finger flexion and extension, palpates the palm-side tendon path, maps joint swelling, and checks sensation and strength. When clinically useful, ultrasound is used selectively to examine the tendon sheath, joint, and nearby soft tissue before conservative care, acupuncture, possible acupotomy, or referral is explained.

Chief Director Donghae Lee first distinguishes finger catching, joint swelling, and nerve symptoms, using ultrasound when needed to examine the tendon sheath, joint, and nearby soft tissue. After the examination, he explains the role of each treatment and whether referral is needed, then reassesses the response using the same fist-making, finger-opening, and gripping tasks.

1. Three patterns to separate first

Palm-side pain with clicking, catching, or locking

Trigger finger can occur when the flexor tendon and A1 pulley no longer glide smoothly. Catching, locking, pain, and morning worsening after inactivity are recognized features.[1]

Warm swelling in several small joints, often on both hands

Inflammatory joint disease needs to be considered. Persistent small-joint swelling, warmth, and morning stiffness should receive timely medical assessment rather than being self-diagnosed by duration alone.[2]

Numbness, sensory loss, or weakness

Wrist-nerve or neck/arm neurological problems require a separate screen. Sudden weakness, deformity after injury, or a hand that becomes cold, pale, or blue requires urgent assessment rather than waiting for a routine booking.

Thirty minutes is not a diagnostic cutoff. The location and number of swollen joints, warmth, symmetry, catching point, sensory or strength change, diabetes, rheumatic disease, and medicines all matter.

2. What Bodyall checks

  • Which joint or palm-side point hurts, and whether one digit or several joints are involved
  • The same fist-making and finger-opening task, noting catching, locking, and range
  • Tendon-path tenderness, joint swelling and warmth, and skin change
  • Sensation, strength, and associated wrist, neck, or arm symptoms
  • Selective ultrasound only when it helps examine the tendon sheath, joint, or nearby soft tissue

Ultrasound findings are recorded alongside the location of catching, joint swelling, and movements the patient finds difficult. Together, these inform the explanation of whether acupuncture, consideration of acupotomy, conservative care, or referral is appropriate.

A 2024 narrative review describes assessing trigger finger through the history, examination, hand function, and ultrasound findings.[3]

3. Treatment roles are kept distinct

Load adjustment and gentle motion

Reduce repeated forceful gripping or pinching and move within a comfortable range. Do not force open a hot, swollen, or locked finger.

Acupuncture

After examination, acupuncture may be considered for symptom and movement management. The same finger-flexion and extension tasks are repeated afterward to assess any change.

Considering acupotomy

If the examination supports a localized tendon problem, acupotomy may be discussed as one invasive option only after conservative choices and other care pathways are explained. It is not routine for every stiff hand; expected benefits, limitations, risks, and alternatives are individualized.

Scope of Chuna and SART

Chuna or SART is considered separately only when there is a relevant neck or arm movement problem. Neither is automatically added as the default treatment for joint swelling or trigger finger.

4. Retest the same function

The pre-treatment record—making a fist, fully opening the hand, gripping a cap, and the location of catching—is repeated in the same way after care or a management trial. If function does not change or swelling, warmth, locking, or neurological symptoms progress, the plan is reassessed rather than simply repeated.
