1. How are frozen shoulder, tendon pain and neck-related symptoms separated?

Clues suggesting adhesive capsulitis

  • Both active and passive motion are restricted, often external rotation and arm elevation
  • Months of pain, night pain and progressive difficulty with daily tasks
  • Relevant conditions such as diabetes or thyroid disease need consideration

Other causes to check

  • Painful active elevation with relatively preserved passive range: possible rotator cuff-related pain
  • Sudden weakness after trauma: possible major tendon injury, fracture or dislocation
  • Arm tingling with neck movement, sensory or reflex change: cervical or peripheral nerve involvement
  • Localized pain on top of the shoulder: possible acromioclavicular-joint problem

No single provocative test is diagnostic, and adhesive-capsulitis tests remain too heterogeneous to provide one scientifically validated set.[2][3]

When testing or urgent assessment comes first

Seek urgent assessment after major trauma if the arm cannot be raised or the shoulder is deformed, for sudden severe pain or marked swelling, persistent numbness or progressive weakness, or a red-hot shoulder with fever. Shoulder or arm pain with chest pressure, breathlessness, sweating or pain spreading to the jaw also needs emergency assessment.[5]

3. Treatment is selected for a verified role

Load guidance and graded exercise

Aggravating work and sleep positions are adjusted while tolerable movement and strengthening are progressed. The 2025 rotator-cuff guideline centers assessment, nonsurgical management and rehabilitation.[1]

Acupuncture and pharmacopuncture

These may be considered for symptom management at examined local sites. They are not presented as regenerating a tendon, eliminating inflammation or preventing recurrence.

Myofascial Chuna

Used when local soft-tissue tension and movement restriction contribute to the painful arm task, with the same task retested.

Corrective Chuna and SART

Not automatic for shoulder pain. They may be adjuncts when modifiable neck, thoracic or scapular movement is linked to shoulder function. This does not mean permanently widening spinal or shoulder space or restoring alignment.

Acupotomy

Considered only after discussion when persistent local restriction or an adhesive-capsulitis pattern is clear and alternatives have been weighed. Bleeding, bruising, infection, and nerve, vessel or tendon injury are possible, so it is not routine care.

The key is not the procedure name but adjusting the plan after checking safety and the same functional task.

4. What the evidence supports—and does not

The 2025 rotator-cuff tendinopathy guideline covers history and examination, nonsurgical care, rehabilitation and return to function.[1] A review of shoulder-pain guidelines also prioritized red flags and clinical examination and generally did not recommend early MRI, but only 12 of 26 guidelines were high quality.[2]

A 2019 acupotomy review analyzed eight RCTs and 501 participants with adhesive capsulitis, but the authors emphasized small samples and low-quality evidence requiring more rigorous trials.[4] This may inform selective discussion; it does not prove immediate adhesion release, guaranteed safety, Bodyall-specific outcomes or superiority over another clinic.

Frequently asked questions

Does difficulty raising my arm prove frozen shoulder?

No. Active and passive range, pain-limited effort versus true weakness, trauma, and neck or neurologic symptoms need comparison.

Is SART required for shoulder pain?

No. Local shoulder findings may favor exercise, local care and myofascial Chuna. Corrective Chuna/SART is an adjunct only when modifiable neck, thoracic or scapular movement is relevant.

Does acupotomy immediately release a stiff capsule?

That cannot be guaranteed. Acupotomy studies have sample-size and evidence-quality limitations, and bleeding, infection and surrounding-tissue injury are possible. Bodyall weighs persistent restriction, alternatives, expected benefit and risk before selective use.
