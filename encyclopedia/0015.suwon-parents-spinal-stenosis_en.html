1. Biomechanical Reasons for Caution Regarding Spinal Stenosis Surgery for Elderly Parents

Spinal stenosis in our parents' generation is the result of degenerative changes over years or decades. As bones and ligaments supporting the spine thicken due to aging, they squeeze the spinal nerve canal leading to the legs from all sides. At this time, fusion surgery that shaves bones and inserts screws can temporarily help secure space, but the mechanical load applied to the segments above and below the surgical site surges by 2 to 3 times, making it easy to trigger a secondary recurrence called 'adjacent segment degeneration syndrome.' Since the risks of general anesthesia and a long recovery period increase with age, non-surgical structural restoration must be considered first.
