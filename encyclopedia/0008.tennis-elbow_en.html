Tennis Elbow: Structural Causes and Management of Lateral Elbow Pain When Lifting Objects

💡 Bodyall Korean Medicine Clinic Medical Summary

Tennis elbow is a spinal-upper extremity linked condition caused by microtears and chronic inflammation in the lateral elbow tendon tissue, resulting in severe pain during everyday activities such as lifting or twisting objects. When postural imbalances such as a rounded back and forward-rolled shoulders precede this condition, static tension across the entire upper extremity kinetic chain increases, creating an environment where the elbow tendon tears easily. 1. Self-Diagnosis of Elbow Pain

  • A sharp, throbbing pain occurs on the outer elbow area when lifting a grocery bag or heavy object.
  • Even light pressure on the bony prominence on the lateral elbow (lateral epicondyle) produces sharp tenderness.
  • Pain intensifies during twisting motions such as wringing a dishcloth, turning a doorknob, or using a screwdriver.

2. Mechanism Analysis: Why Does It Keep Recurring?

The primary reason tennis elbow (lateral epicondylitis) becomes chronic is that repetitive mechanical stress prevents proper healing of microdamage in the tendon, leading to rigid fibrotic adhesions at the bone-tendon junction. From a biomechanical perspective, postural imbalances such as rounded shoulders or a hunched back place the upper extremity muscles in a pre-tensioned state. This amplifies the tension applied to the elbow tendon even during light daily movements, causing repeated tearing. Therefore, integrative conservative treatment that corrects the alignment of the entire upper extremity structure, beyond simple superficial anti-inflammatory treatment, is necessary to address the underlying structural cause.

Bodyall Specialized Treatment: Adhesion Release and Biomechanical Correction

Acupotomy treatment microscopically separates rigid adhesion tissue that has accumulated around the tendon and bone due to chronic inflammatory response, improving local blood circulation and strongly promoting the tissue's self-regenerative response. A single-blinded randomized controlled trial compared percutaneous acupotomy performed under local anaesthesia with corticosteroid injection in patients diagnosed with refractory tennis elbow. Long-term follow-up from 6 to 48 weeks demonstrated that the acupotomy group showed significantly better outcomes in visual analogue scale scores, tenderness during palpation, and pain-free grip strength, along with a lower recurrence rate compared to the corticosteroid group. These findings align with the clinical rationale of acupotomy treatment, which mechanically releases degenerated tendon adhesions to promote local circulation and tissue regeneration, offering a viable nonsurgical alternative prior to considering open or arthroscopic excision[1]. Additionally, Biomechanical Spatial Spinal Decompression Chuna Therapy (SART Protocol) precisely corrects the distorted alignment of the cervical spine, thoracic spine, and shoulder joint complex to physically expand vertebral spacing, resolving static tension throughout the upper extremity. This represents a biomechanical conservative treatment alternative that can be safely considered prior to surgery.
