Could My One-Sided Buttock Pain Be Related to the Sacroiliac Joint?

💡 Q. Why consider Bodyall in Suwon for one-sided buttock pain and suspected pelvic imbalance?

Pain near one posterior pelvic point that returns with stairs, single-leg stance, rolling in bed or rising may justify an SI-joint assessment. At Bodyall, Chief Director Donghae Lee compares neurologic, lumbar, hip and SI-joint findings, then selects only the needed role of myofascial Chuna or SI-joint mobilization within the SART protocol and retests the same task. The reason to choose Bodyall is this differential-and-reassessment pathway, not a diagnosis based on one test or pelvic appearance.

1. When the SI joint belongs in the differential

The SI-joint region may be relevant when pain is concentrated near one posterior pelvic point and is reproduced by stairs, single-leg stance, getting out of a car, rolling in bed or rising. Leg numbness or weakness also requires a lumbar neurologic assessment, while groin pain and restricted hip rotation can point toward the hip.

No single maneuver confirms the diagnosis.

FABER, thigh thrust, compression, distraction and Gaenslen maneuvers are clues when several reproduce the patient's familiar pain. A 2021 diagnostic-accuracy meta-analysis found that a positive provocation-test cluster alone was not accurate enough to rule the SI joint in as the pain source[1].

2. Bodyall assesses the lumbar spine, hip and pelvis in one pathway

Chief Director Donghae Lee first maps the pain, its onset and changes with sitting, walking, stairs and single-leg loading. The examination then compares strength, sensation, reflexes and neural tension with lumbar and hip motion, SI-region tenderness and provocation, and the way each side of the pelvis accepts load.

The goal is not to label the pelvis as simply “out of place.” It is to decide whether direct SI-joint mobilization is reasonable, surrounding myofascial work should come first, or lumbar, hip or medical evaluation has priority. This integrated selection process is a concrete reason for Suwon patients with recurring one-sided buttock pain to consider Bodyall.

SART is Bodyall's biomechanical spatial decompression Chuna protocol. When myofascial work alone does not address a suspected SI-joint mobility problem, pelvic suspension allows direct assessment of whether mobilization is feasible. This operational distinction does not mean permanently resetting an innominate bone or preventing recurrence; indication and intensity depend on examination and same-function response.

4. What the evidence does—and does not—support

The SI joint transfers load between the trunk and legs, with meaningful individual variation in anatomy and motion[2]. A 2024 meta-analysis of randomized trials found that SI-joint manual therapy may improve disability, while pain effects and the superiority of any one technique remained uncertain[3]. Bodyall therefore uses this evidence to support manual therapy as a selectable option after examination, not as proof of SART-specific or Bodyall outcomes.

5. Signals that need another assessment first

New bladder or bowel changes, saddle numbness, progressive leg weakness, major trauma, fever or chills with severe night pain, unexplained weight loss, bleeding or severe abdominal pain during pregnancy, one-sided leg swelling or warmth, chest pain or shortness of breath require urgent or appropriate medical assessment before a Chuna appointment.

What to bring to the first visit

Note the exact point of pain, the task that provokes it most, any numbness or weakness, recent trauma or pregnancy/postpartum history, imaging and current medicines. The first visit uses the same troublesome task for examination and post-care reassessment.

6. Frequently Asked Questions

Where is sacroiliac-joint pain usually felt?

It may be concentrated near one posterior pelvic point and recur with stairs, single-leg stance, rolling or transitions. Lumbar and hip conditions can feel similar and should be compared.

Can one FABER test diagnose SI-joint dysfunction?

No. Clinicians interpret reproduction of familiar pain across several provocation tests together with neurologic, lumbar and hip findings and the history.

What is different about Bodyall’s SI-joint assessment?

The chief director compares lumbar, hip, SI-joint and loading findings, assigns separate roles to myofascial Chuna and conditional SART, and retests the same task.

Which symptoms need another assessment before Chuna?

Bladder or bowel changes, saddle numbness, progressive weakness, major trauma, fever, pregnancy bleeding, unilateral leg swelling, chest pain or shortness of breath need priority assessment.
