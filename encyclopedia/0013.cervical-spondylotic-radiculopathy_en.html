Arm tingling when you turn your neck: is an MRI report of “foraminal stenosis” enough?

What findings raise suspicion of cervical radiculopathy?

Degenerative loss of disc height, osteophytes and facet changes may narrow the passage used by a cervical nerve root. Neck or scapular pain may then travel into an arm or hand with altered sensation, weakness or reduced reflexes. These patterns overlap, so numbness in one finger does not establish the affected level by itself.

Is the Spurling test diagnostic?

No. A 2025 meta-analysis reported pooled sensitivity of 0.53 and specificity of 0.92. A positive result can support the diagnosis, but a negative result does not reliably exclude it; history, neurological examination and appropriate imaging still matter.[1] A prospective diagnostic study likewise cautioned against relying on any single interview item or test.[2]

Chief Director Lee checks whether the image finding matches the function that is actually limited: the pain or tingling path, strength, sensation, reflexes, neck rotation and extension, shoulder movement and peripheral-nerve provocation are compared in one visit. This helps distinguish a nerve-root problem from shoulder disease or peripheral nerve entrapment while screening for progressive deficit and myelopathy.

Bodyall does not claim that Chuna or SART removes osteophytes or physically enlarges the neural foramen. The aim is to address modifiable movement and soft-tissue findings identified on examination and observe the response of the same symptom and task. A 2025 review found that high-quality neck-pain guidelines recommend manipulation or mobilisation, with or without exercise, but this does not establish a SART-specific effect or structural decompression.[3]

Signs that need another assessment first

Rapidly worsening arm or hand weakness, loss of hand dexterity, gait or balance change, symptoms in both arms or legs, or bowel and bladder change require prompt assessment for myelopathy or another urgent cause. Major trauma, fever or unexplained weight loss, and severe headache, dizziness or speech disturbance also take priority over cervical manual therapy.

Key points

  • One Spurling test or MRI finding does not determine the diagnosis or treatment.
  • Neurological, shoulder and peripheral-nerve findings are separated before conservative care is selected.
  • Myofascial Chuna and corrective Chuna/SART have distinct roles, followed by same-task reassessment.
  • No claim is made that treatment removes osteophytes, enlarges the foramen or prevents recurrence.
