1. Sleep positions are clues, not self-diagnosis

  • Whether pain begins while lying flat, lying on your side or turning
  • Whether straightening the legs also causes tingling or a sense of weakness
  • How much knee support or a position change improves comfort
  • How long morning stiffness lasts and how movement changes it

Describe onset, injuries, conditions and medicines, awakenings at night, and symptoms during walking or sitting. A response to a pillow or the gap under your back does not demonstrate a specific tissue contracture or adhesion.

2. Distinguish back, leg and hip problems and warning signs

Back pain has varied causes, including muscle, joint, disc and nerve problems; sometimes the cause remains unclear. Fracture, infection or cancer occasionally requires a different evaluation.[3] History, neurological symptoms and back, hip and pelvic movement need to be considered together.

Bodyall’s described palpation and active/passive movement assessment separates soft-tissue tension from restricted joint movement and helps judge whether corrective Chuna is suitable.[6] Bring existing test results. Imaging is selected when it could change management, not routinely repeated for everyone.[4]

3. How are Chuna, SART and acupotomy selected?

Myofascial versus conditional corrective Chuna/SART

Myofascial-release Chuna addresses soft-tissue tension and uncomfortable back/pelvic movement. Corrective Chuna and biomechanical spatial decompression Chuna (SART Protocol) are selected for suitability and intensity when examination indicates joint movement and load-management needs.[6] Bodyall uses the examination to select the site, direction and intensity for back and pelvic joint movement and load management.

Manual treatment is planned alongside exercise and daily-activity management.[4] Disclose injuries, osteoporosis, medicines and other risk factors, and discuss alternatives and risks. Temporary soreness or stiffness and rare serious events such as nerve injury have been reported.[5]

Acupotomy is a separate, conditional decision

Before considering acupotomy, clarify the target, need and alternatives such as exercise or other conservative care. Pain when lying down does not establish deep iliopsoas or perineural adhesions. Discuss bleeding, infection, nerve, vessel and surrounding-tissue injury and site-specific risks. Acupuncture and pharmacopuncture are not automatic additions; bee venom requires separate consideration of severe allergy and anaphylaxis.

Recheck the same movement

Reassess the original painful action—lying down, turning or getting up. Bodyall’s care description includes movement reassessment after treatment.[6] Share a record of sleep awakenings, pain and medicine use at follow-up. Lack of improvement or progressing neurological symptoms calls for reconsidering diagnosis, treatment and the need for further tests or specialist assessment.

4. What can the existing acupotomy studies tell us?

The 2013 study compared before/after outcomes in 437 inpatients with lumbar stenosis receiving acupotomy plus other Korean medicine treatments. With no control group, it cannot establish acupotomy’s isolated effect or structural restoration.[1]

The 2025 review analyzed 14 randomized trials and reported possible pain/function benefits in some comparisons; only five studies reported safety.[2] Both concern stenosis, not all nighttime back pain or Bodyall’s SART outcomes. They inform treatment discussion after assessment.

5. Comfortable positions and tolerable activity before your visit

Do not force a painful position. Find a comfortable one and remain active as tolerated rather than staying in bed for long periods. Stop exercises that worsen pain and seek advice. Wrap a heat pack in a towel rather than applying it directly to skin.[3] A particular lunge or forceful stretch is not a universal prescription.

6. When assessment takes priority over Chuna booking

New bladder/bowel dysfunction, saddle numbness, severe neurological symptoms in both legs, chest pain or pain after a major accident needs emergency assessment; in Korea call 119 or attend an emergency department. Fever/chills or rapidly worsening pain also requires prompt care. Report persistent rest/night pain, unexplained weight loss or a cancer history when arranging care.[3]

Frequently asked questions

Does pain lying flat that eases with bent knees mean stenosis?

That response alone cannot diagnose stenosis or iliopsoas shortening. Pain timing, positional changes, leg neurological symptoms and examination must be considered together. Pain persisting at rest or worsening at night should be assessed first.

No. Bodyall describes history, palpation and active/passive movement assessment to distinguish suitability for myofascial-release versus corrective Chuna/SART. Acupotomy is a separate choice after discussing its need, alternatives and risks.
