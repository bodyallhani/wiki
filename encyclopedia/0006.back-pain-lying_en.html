Back Self-Diagnosis Guide

"Back pain every night—why is it so hard even to lie flat on my back?"

💡 30-Second Key Summary from Bodyall Clinic

1. Pain when lying flat is often due to spinal canal compression or excessive tension in the iliopsoas muscle.

2. If you only feel comfortable sleeping curled on your side, spinal deformation (stenosis, etc.) is likely progressing.

3. Bodyall helps you sleep soundly by opening spinal space with SART and relaxing the iliopsoas.

1. Sleep Posture & Pain Checklist

Some people want to lie down because they're tired, but their back hurts like it's breaking once they do. Check your symptoms.

  • Sharp pain occurs as my lower back arches up when I lie flat on the bed.
  • Lying with legs straight causes numbness or weakness throughout my legs.
  • Pain only subsides when I prop up my knees or lie curled on my side.
  • Immediately after waking up, it's hard to straighten my back and I have to move awkwardly for a while.

2. Mechanism: Why does lying flat hurt?

Back pain when lying flat is largely due to two structural defects.

① Spinal Canal Compression: Lying straight narrows the spinal canal, the nerve pathway. If there is stenosis, the nerves are compressed, causing intense pain.
② Iliopsoas Shortening: An iliopsoas muscle shortened by prolonged sitting habits pulls the lumbar vertebrae forward when lying down, creating an excessive 'back arch (lordosis)'.

3. Bodyall's Total Care Solution

The key is creating 'space' where nerves are not pinched and resolving 'tension' that pulls on the spine.

① Specialized Care: Restoring Space

• SART / Chuna Therapy: We widen narrowed spinal segments to relieve nerve pressure and realign the pelvis to resolve lumbar hyperlordosis.
• Acupotomy: Minimally separates the hardened iliopsoas and surrounding ligaments to immediately increase spinal flexibility.

② Korean Medicine Basis: Inflammation & Relaxation

• Comprehensive Solution: Acupuncture and Bee Venom Therapy reduce swelling around nerves, while cupping aids deep blood circulation.
• Modern PT: ICT and TENS relax the erector spinae muscles and stabilize pain nerves.

4. Emergency Tips for a Comfortable Sleep

  • Pillow Under Knees: Try placing a thick pillow under your knees when lying flat. This alleviates the lumbar curve and feels much more comfortable.
  • Warm Compress: Applying a warm pack under your lower back before bed helps fully relax the muscles.
  • Lunge Stretching: Light stretching to lengthen a shortened iliopsoas reduces back compression.
