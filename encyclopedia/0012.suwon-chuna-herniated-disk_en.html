Why Pain Persists Despite Frequent Nerve Blocks and Manual Therapy

A common clinical challenge for herniated disk patients is undergoing repetitive injection therapies or standard manual therapy without addressing underlying structural imbalances. Nerve blocks function primarily by reducing localized inflammation and washing away biochemical irritants around the nerve roots; however, they do not physically counteract the axial compression between vertebral bodies that continuously compresses and compromises the intervertebral disc. Unless adequate physical space is restored between the osseous structures, the disc material will continue to displace and impinge upon adjacent neural pathways.

However, when deep stabilizing ligaments and segmental spinal articulations have undergone long-term contracture and rigid fixation, the depth and vector of conventional manual force are often insufficient to restore segment mobility.

Checklist for Chronic Herniated Disk Progression Stages

  • Radiating pain, paresthesia, or tingling that travels from the lumbar region through the gluteal area, descending down the lower extremity to the digits.
  • Exacerbation of lower back discomfort during prolonged ambulation or static standing, leading to severe fatigue and an urge to sit down immediately.
  • Transient symptomatic relief from therapeutic injections or general physical therapy that fails to sustain improvement beyond forty-eight to seventy-two hours.
