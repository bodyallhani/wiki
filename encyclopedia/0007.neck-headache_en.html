Headache with a stiff neck: what does Bodyall assess first?

1. Describe these symptoms at your consultation

  • Stiffness at the back of the neck with pain spreading toward the temples or side of the head
  • Neck tension and headache that increase after prolonged computer or smartphone use
  • Your usual headache reproduced by a particular neck movement, with restricted movement

Also describe when headaches began, their frequency and duration, sensitivity to light or sound, nausea, medicines and previous test results. New visual disturbance or marked eye pain needs prompt assessment, not an assumption of neck fatigue.[4]

2. A posture image does not diagnose cervicogenic headache

The International Classification of Headache Disorders requires evidence linking a cervical disorder to the headache and consideration of other diagnoses. Restricted movement and headache provocation are clues, not a stand-alone diagnostic test. Degenerative imaging findings can also occur without headache.[1]

Bodyall’s assessment connects symptoms and history with neck/shoulder tenderness and active and passive movement to identify the problem to address.[6] Migraine, tension-type headache, occipital neuralgia and neurological or vascular problems may need consideration. A response to one treatment does not confirm the diagnosis or replace indicated testing or specialist care.

3. How are Chuna and SART selected at Bodyall?

Separate soft-tissue tension from joint-movement restriction

Myofascial-release Chuna addresses tension and uncomfortable movement around the neck and shoulders. Corrective Chuna and biomechanical spatial decompression Chuna (SART Protocol) are selected when examination indicates a need to address joint movement and posture/loading, with suitability and intensity determined individually. SART is Bodyall’s named Chuna care protocol, not a necessary treatment for every headache.[6]

Do not repeatedly force or crack your own neck. Before cervical manual treatment, discuss trauma, underlying conditions and medicines, as well as alternatives and risks. Temporary pain or stiffness and rare serious neurological or vascular events have been reported.[5]

Reassess against the same starting point

After treatment, the initially uncomfortable neck rotation and daily movement are checked again.[6] Bring a record of headache days, intensity, medication days and symptoms during computer work to follow-up. Discuss exercise and adjustments to working position and breaks. If symptoms do not improve or their pattern changes, the diagnosis and plan should be reconsidered.

Acupuncture, acupotomy and bee-venom pharmacopuncture are separate decisions. Acupotomy is not presented as a guaranteed solution to the headache’s cause or nerve compression. It carries risks of bleeding, infection and injury to nerves, vessels and surrounding tissues; bee venom can cause severe allergy and anaphylaxis. Each requires a separate discussion of need, alternatives, risks and consent. A headache alone is not a reason to select a bundle of additional procedures.

4. Evidence that informs the choice—and its limits

A 2022 systematic review found potential symptom benefits from manual and exercise therapies for cervicogenic headache. Many studies had high risk of bias; analysis restricted to low-risk trials found small manual-therapy effects.[3]

The previously cited 2011 review found mixed results and uncertainty, with no superiority in the adequately placebo-controlled trial.[2] These findings help inform treatment selection and response monitoring. They do not demonstrate SART-specific nerve decompression, restoration of the cervical curve or superior headache outcomes at Bodyall.

5. Do not wait for a routine appointment with these symptoms

Sudden extreme headache, facial or limb weakness, difficulty speaking or walking, altered consciousness or a seizure, loss of vision, high fever with a stiff neck, or headache after a head injury require emergency assessment. In Korea, call 119 or attend an emergency department. New visual/eye problems, jaw pain when chewing or scalp tenderness, and headaches worsened by coughing or straining also need prompt medical attention.[4]

Frequently asked questions

Does headache with forward-head posture mean I need SART?

No. Neck pain and posture alone cannot establish cervicogenic headache. The headache pattern and neck/neurological assessment guide whether myofascial-release Chuna, conditional corrective Chuna/SART, exercise and daily-activity management, or other care is appropriate.

Why consider Bodyall Korean Medicine Clinic in Suwon for a consultation?

For a headache recurring with neck movement, Bodyall describes a connected process of history, palpation and range-of-motion assessment, individualized Chuna type and intensity, and reassessment of the same movement after treatment. Its Ingye-dong location near Suwon City Hall Station and weekday hours until 20:30 also support planning follow-up care.

Can I stop pain medicine and use Chuna alone?

Do not stop or increase medicine on your own. Bring the names and days of use, and discuss changes with the prescribing clinician. A headache diary can help assess treatment response and possible medication overuse.
