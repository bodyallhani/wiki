1. Characteristics of Cervicogenic Headaches That Do Not Improve with Medication Alone

  • Stiffness in the back of the neck and nape, accompanied by pain radiating toward the temples or lateral head region
  • Ocular pain or blurred vision-related fatigue occurring concurrently with headache episodes
  • Intensified neck tension and headache severity immediately following prolonged computer or smartphone use

2. Biomechanical Mechanism Analysis: How Forward Head Deformity Induces Headaches

When the neck loses its normal C-shaped lordotic curve, the weight of the head becomes concentrated on the upper cervical segments. This causes the deep occipital muscles to become excessively tense, compressing the occipital nerves and consequently triggering sharp or throbbing neuropathic pain. A systematic review examining Chuna manual therapy for cervicogenic dizziness reports that structural misalignment of the upper cervical segments is closely associated with dizziness and head-related neurological symptoms[1]. This body of research suggests that manual therapy approaches aimed at restoring cervical alignment may be considered as a conservative treatment option worthy of academic consideration in managing cervicogenic symptoms. The Biomechanical Spatial Spinal Decompression Chuna Therapy (SART Protocol) applied at this clinic is based on the principle of securing physical space between upper cervical segments to address structural misalignment, which academically aligns with the concept of addressing the structural causes of cervicogenic neurological symptoms discussed in this research. This condition should be managed through solutions aimed at improving the fundamental structural causes of degenerative spinal diseases.
