💡 Key Summary Analyzed by Medical Editors

Piriformis Syndrome is a biomechanical abnormality in which the deep pelvic muscle compresses the sciatic nerve, causing radiating pain into the lower extremities.

While clinically similar to lumbar disc herniation, its main characteristics are severe tenderness deep within the buttock and numbness that worsens specifically when sitting, rather than lumbar spine issues themselves.

Self-Diagnosis for Piriformis Syndrome: Clinical Differentiation

If you are experiencing discomfort in daily life due to persistent numbness along the outer buttock and posterior thigh without distinct pain in the lower back itself, it is highly likely that this is a state of nerve compression exceeding simple muscle fatigue. If you meet three or more of the following clinical signs, a precise biomechanical examination is recommended.

  • A sharp, tingling neuralgia gradually rises from deep within the buttock during long-term driving or sitting continuously in an office chair for more than 30 minutes.
  • Numbness intensifies as the hip external rotator muscles are stimulated when sitting comfortably in a cross-legged position on the floor or habitually crossing legs in a chair.
  • Pressing firmly on the central area of the affected buttock, where the sciatic nerve passes, induces startling radiating pain and extreme tenderness.
  • Pain intensifies as the tension in the internal pelvic muscles temporarily increases when turning over or changing positions in a specific direction during nighttime sleep.
  • A strong pulling symptom occurs in the back of the thigh as the gluteus maximus and piriformis contract while climbing stairs or walking on a steep incline.

Pain Mechanism from a Biomechanical Perspective

The Piriformis muscle, located deep within the pelvis, is a key muscle that maintains hip joint stability. Directly beneath this muscle runs the sciatic nerve, the largest nerve network in the human body. Due to spinal and pelvic imbalance, or continuous poor posture, if the piriformis muscle becomes abnormally hypertonic or hypertrophied, nerve compression causing pain occurs continuously.

Clinical Differences Between Lumbar Disc Herniation and Piriformis Syndrome

The two conditions are easily confused as they share radiating pain in the lower extremities, but they show clear differences depending on the fundamental anatomical location where the nerve is compressed.
