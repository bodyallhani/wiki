Piriformis Syndrome Self-Diagnosis: Analyzing the Causes of Buttock Pain and Leg Numbness

💡 Key Summary Analyzed by the Medical Editor

Piriformis syndrome is a biomechanical abnormality in which a deep pelvic muscle compresses the sciatic nerve, resulting in radiating pain down the lower extremity.

Although clinically similar to lumbar disc herniation, it is primarily characterized by severe tenderness deep within the buttock and numbness that worsens upon sitting, rather than pain originating from the lumbar spine itself.

Based on an analysis of clinical cases from patients visiting our main clinic in Ingye-dong, Suwon, as well as from surrounding areas such as Gwanggyo, Yeongtong, and Dongtan, correcting the alignment of the misaligned pelvis and spinal segments through conservative treatment is essential for a solution aimed at improving the fundamental structural causes of degenerative spinal diseases.

Clinical Differentiation via Piriformis Syndrome Self-Diagnosis

If you are experiencing discomfort in daily life due to persistent numbness along the lateral buttock and posterior lower extremity without distinct lumbar pain, this may indicate a state of nerve compression beyond simple muscle fatigue. If three or more of the following clinical signs apply, a precise biomechanical evaluation is recommended.

  • A sharp, deep neuralgic sensation gradually rises from within the buttock during prolonged driving or sitting in an office chair for more than 30 minutes.
  • Numbness worsens due to stimulation of the hip external rotators when sitting cross-legged on the floor or habitually crossing the legs while seated in a chair.
  • Applying firm pressure to the central point of the affected buttock, where the sciatic nerve passes, elicits a startling radiating pain and severe tenderness.
  • Pain intensifies during sleep when turning to a specific side or changing position, as the tension in the internal pelvic muscles temporarily increases.
  • A strong pulling sensation manifests in the posterior thigh as the gluteus maximus and piriformis contract while climbing stairs or walking up a steep incline.

The Mechanism of Pain Onset from a Biomechanical Perspective

The piriformis muscle, located deep within the pelvis, is a key muscle that maintains hip joint stability. Directly beneath this muscle runs the sciatic nerve, the thickest nerve network in the human body. When the piriformis becomes abnormally hypertonic or hypertrophied due to spinal-pelvic imbalance or persistent poor posture, it results in continuous nerve compression that induces pain.

Clinical Differences Between Lumbar Disc Herniation and Piriformis Syndrome

Since both conditions share radiating pain down the lower extremity, they are easily confused; however, they exhibit clear differences depending on the fundamental anatomical location of nerve compression.
