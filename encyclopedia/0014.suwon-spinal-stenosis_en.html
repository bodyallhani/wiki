1. Spinal Stenosis Recommended for Surgery, Why Is Treatment So Difficult?

Unlike disc diseases where the disc protrudes, spinal stenosis (Spinal Stenosis) is a degenerative disease where the spinal bone thickens with age and the ligaments inside the spinal canal swell abnormally, causing the nerve pathway itself to be squeezed from all sides. A representative symptom is 'intermittent claudication,' where the legs ache as if they are about to burst after walking even a little, requiring rest. Since physical therapy or painkillers that simply soothe inflammation cannot re-expand a structurally blocked tunnel (nerve canal), many patients receive a diagnosis that surgery is the only answer.
