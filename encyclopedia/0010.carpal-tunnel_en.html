Is it Carpal Tunnel Syndrome? Self-Diagnosis

Many patients traveling from Suwon Ingye-dong, as well as premium catchment areas like Gwanggyo, Yeongtong, and Dongtan, complain of progressive paresthesia. Check if any of the following clinical signs apply to you:

  • My hands tingle and go numb at night, disrupting sleep cycles and prompting an instinctive shaking motion to restore blood flow.
  • Numbness or diminished sensation spreads across the thumb, index, middle, and half of the ring finger.[1]
  • Grip strength decreases unexpectedly, leading to dropped objects or difficulty managing fine motor tasks like buttoning a shirt.[2]
  • Tapping over the palmar aspect of the wrist produces an electric shock-like sensation radiating to the fingertips (positive Tinel's sign).

Mechanism: Why do the fingers go numb?

The carpal tunnel is an osseofibrous canal in the wrist through which the median nerve passes. Repetitive strain triggers ligamentous hypertrophy and soft tissue thickening, elevating internal canal pressure and squeezing the nerve.[1] Crucially, structural alterations such as forward head posture (turtle neck) trigger multi-segmental kinetic chain imbalances, making nerve roots increasingly hypersensitive and compounding peripheral entrapment.[2] This comprehensive diagnostic view explains why individuals from Suwon Ingye-dong, alongside Gwanggyo, Yeongtong, and Dongtan, travel to seek targeted non-surgical structural normalization.
