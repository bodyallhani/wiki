How Bodyall connects treatment to aftercare

We do not apply a single rule such as “always rest” or “stop every exercise.” The next step is chosen by comparing symptoms and function under the same conditions.

1

Check change and risk

Review pain location and intensity, numbness, strength, walking, and bladder or bowel changes. Warning signs take priority over self-management.

2

Separate the roles

Chuna or SART, when selected after examination, is in-clinic care. Posture, activity pacing, and exercise support daily management; they are not self-administered correction.

3

Reassess the same function

Compare sitting, walking, bending, or neck turning under the same conditions. If the result worsens or stalls, the plan can be reconsidered in clinic.

Seek medical assessment before following an exercise guide if you notice

New or progressive arm or leg weakness, saddle numbness, or bladder or bowel dysfunction; severe pain after major trauma; severe pain with fever; chest pain or shortness of breath. Rapid worsening after treatment should also be reported to Bodyall promptly.

Frequently asked questions

Which guide should I read first?

If pain is new or worsening, check warning signs and acute pain guidance first and contact Bodyall. If symptoms are stable, proceed from precautions to posture, activity, and exercise guidance.

Is all discomfort after correction normal?

No. Causes and severity vary. New weakness, sensory change, or bladder or bowel dysfunction requires prompt medical assessment.

Does home care replace an in-clinic reassessment?

No. Home management is supportive. The same painful movement and function may need to be reassessed in clinic so treatment choices can be adjusted.
