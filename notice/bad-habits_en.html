[Habits and Postures to Avoid]

In terms of spinal stability and disc protection, poor posture directly disrupts spinal alignment. Even if there are no problems when you are healthy, small irritations can lead to severe pain during correction treatment, so please be careful with the following items.

❌ Primary Postures to Avoid

1. Bending your neck or back downward
2. Slumping or slouching in a chair
3. Sitting cross-legged on the floor
** Please do not maintain or repeat these postures for more than 15 minutes.

Precautions in Daily Life

1. Alcohol (Caution until recovered)

Drinking enough to get intoxicated within 2 weeks after pain has subsided or while it persists can cause a sudden relapse of severe pain.

2. Hiking and Uphill Paths

Climbing stairs for a long time or hiking forces the lower back bones to bend backward (kyphosis) due to human anatomy, increasing the risk of re-misalignment. Please enjoy these after your health is restored.

3. Long Drives Without Cushions or Rest

Driving for more than 20 minutes places a very high burden on spinal alignment and intradiscal pressure. Since car seats can cause misalignment if used without support, make sure to use a cushion and take breaks for BodyAll Stretching whenever possible.

4. Sitting for Over 20 Minutes Without Pelvic Rolling

Sitting is the position where it is easiest for the back and waist to slouch. In the early stages of treatment, you must support your back with a cushion. Later, you should practice creating space between vertebrae through pelvic rolling. It is also best to avoid crossing your legs when sitting.

5. The Habit of Constant Tension for "Proper Posture"

Excessive obsession with proper posture can make the body more tense and uncomfortable. We will take care of the spinal correction (hardware). Patients only need to relax and mind the guidelines we provided.

However, please pay attention if your body balance is significantly collapsed, such as when leaning on one leg. Brief instances are not a problem, but long-term repetition can cause misalignment.
Proper posture is not something created by forcing the body, but something that completes itself naturally once the spinal position changes and the method is re-learned. Never use excessive force!
