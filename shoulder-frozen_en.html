💡 Q. Why does shoulder pain keep recurring even after treatment?

When the upper back becomes rounded, the scapula is pushed forward, causing the shoulder muscles to remain stretched and stiffened. Once structural alignment collapses, the internal space of the glenohumeral joint narrows, resulting in persistent impingement friction during movement. Therefore, correcting the kyphotic upper back alongside simple inflammation relief is essential to reducing the tension placed on the muscles.

1. Understanding Shoulder Pain Through the Principle of Tension

  • Cause Analysis: A kyphotic thoracic spine induces anterior displacement of the scapula, pulling surrounding soft tissues to their limit.
  • Anatomical Consequence: Tendons under sustained tension experience intensified friction even with minor movement, easily progressing to adhesive capsulitis or rotator cuff injury.
  • Structural Solution: Alongside localized treatment of the inflamed area, correcting thoracic kyphosis to relieve tension in the muscles surrounding the scapula is key to lowering the risk of recurrence.

4. Non-Surgical Conservative Treatment FAQ for Shoulder Pain

💡 Q. What is the biomechanical impact of correcting a kyphotic upper back on shoulder pain treatment?

As thoracic kyphosis intensifies, the scapula is displaced anteriorly, causing the tension of the rotator cuff muscle group to reach a critical threshold. The Biomechanical Spatial Spinal Decompression Chuna Therapy (SART Protocol) correction at Bodyall Korean Medicine Clinic resolves this structural tightness, reducing joint friction and deep inflammation while helping restore the normal range of motion of the shoulder.

💡 Q. What role does acupotomy adhesion release play in the treatment of frozen shoulder?

A fine acupotomy needle is applied to the joint capsule segments hardened by adhesive capsulitis to precisely dissect chronic adhesion sites. In a prospective, double-blind, randomized controlled study involving 63 frozen shoulder patients, the treatment group receiving ultrasound-guided hydrodilatation combined with acupotomy therapy demonstrated significantly superior results in active range of motion and Constant-Murley Scale scores compared to the group receiving hydrodilatation alone. Notably, the combined treatment group showed reduced coracohumeral ligament (CHL) thickness and a lower rate of hypoechoic rotator cuff space thickening compared to the control group, objectively confirming histological adhesion improvement via ultrasound imaging; the overall effective rate was also significantly higher in the combined treatment group at 93.94 percent versus 76.67 percent in the control group, suggesting that acupotomy therapy plays an adjunctive role in resolving joint capsule adhesion[2]. This contributes to immediate mobility restoration as a biomechanical conservative treatment alternative that can be safely selected prior to the invasive surgical stage.
