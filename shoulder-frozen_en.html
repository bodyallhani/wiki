When prompt medical assessment comes first

Seek prompt assessment if you cannot lift the arm after a fall or injury, the shoulder is deformed or severely swollen, fever accompanies severe pain, or arm sensation is lost or persistently altered.[5] Chest pressure or pain spreading to the arm with breathlessness, sweating or dizziness requires emergency help; call 119 in Korea.[6]

1. Is every stiff shoulder frozen shoulder?

Not every painful, stiff shoulder is frozen shoulder. Frozen shoulder, also called adhesive capsulitis, typically restricts both the movement you make yourself and movement assisted by an examiner. Rotator-cuff disease, arthritis, calcific tendinitis and symptoms referred from the neck also need to be distinguished.[3][4]

Frozen shoulder involves thickening and stiffness of the capsule around the joint. Its cause is not fully understood. Diabetes, thyroid disease and prolonged immobility after surgery or trauma may be associated. A rounded upper back or shoulder shape alone does not establish the cause of frozen shoulder or a deep adhesion.[3]

  • What the examination checks: which movement hurts, how active and passive ranges differ, strength, and sensory change in the neck and arm.
  • Role of imaging: frozen shoulder is assessed mainly from history and examination. Ultrasound or MRI is not diagnostic by itself; imaging may help when another condition is suspected.[4]

How does Bodyall choose Chuna, MET or SART?

The examination compares passive shoulder movement and strength, and whether moving the neck or upper back changes symptoms. One or two difficult tasks—dressing, hair washing or reaching behind the back—and night-time sleep form the starting point.

  • Shoulder-joint Chuna: joint restriction and current pain response guide the direction, range and intensity of mobilisation. Forceful movement through severe pain is avoided.
  • Myofascial Chuna and MET: relaxation methods may be selected when surrounding muscle tension limits movement. MET involves the patient's muscle contraction and relaxation within a tolerable, cooperative range.
  • Conditional corrective Chuna and SART: when neck or thoracic joint restriction is also found and there are no contraindications, the clinician selects joint mobilisation, manual correction and, where appropriate, correction tools. Site, direction, intensity and tool use follow examination.
  • Reassess the same function: repeat the same arm raise or reach behind the back and compare the pain response. Follow changes in dressing and night-time sleep to adjust exercises and subsequent care.

Bodyall provides ultrasound and explains its purpose and target when examination suggests another cause, such as a rotator-cuff problem. Ultrasound alone does not diagnose frozen shoulder. Suspected fracture, tear or another problem may require further testing or specialist care.[4]

Temporary soreness or increased pain can follow manual care. New weakness, altered sensation or persistent worsening calls for reassessment before repeating treatment.

3. Which treatment options can be considered?

The plan aims to control pain and restore daily function through range-of-motion exercise, stretching guidance and follow-up matched to current irritability. A 2025 Korean guideline conditionally recommends options including manual therapy with range-of-motion exercise, intra-articular steroid injection and physical therapy. Hydrodilatation may also be considered with steroid injection. Evidence certainty for several recommendations is low, so individual selection is needed.[4]

  • Movement and daily activity: keep the shoulder moving within a tolerable range rather than immobilising it, but avoid forceful painful stretching or heavy equipment. Range and frequency should follow examination.[5]
  • Pain control and medical care: discuss analgesics, anti-inflammatory medicines or injection treatment with the responsible clinician in light of medical conditions and current medicines. Orthopaedic or rehabilitation evaluation may be needed.
  • If improvement is insufficient: reassess night pain, sleep disruption and limits in dressing or hair washing. Persistent major disability despite conservative care may require specialist discussion of additional procedures or surgery.[3]

A 2019 systematic review pooled eight randomised trials with 501 participants. Compared with control groups, acupotomy groups had a mean 0.97-point lower pain VAS and an 8.46-point higher Constant–Murley shoulder-function score. The included trials were small, pain results were highly heterogeneous, and allocation concealment and adverse-event reporting were limited.[1]

 Acupotomy consultation

Discuss the need and site, response to prior care, and risks of bleeding, infection and injury to nerves, blood vessels or nearby tissue. Unlike ordinary acupuncture, acupotomy is an invasive procedure using a bladed needle.

Before considering bee-venom pharmacopuncture, disclose bee-venom and injection allergies and any previous reaction. A previous uneventful treatment or negative skin response does not remove the risk of hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis. Breathing difficulty, widespread hives or dizziness after treatment requires immediate medical attention.[7] Each treatment is selected through its own assessment of need and risk.

4. Frozen shoulder and shoulder-pain FAQ

Q. Does frozen shoulder resolve on its own?

It may improve over time, but recovery can be prolonged and some people retain pain or movement restriction. When sleep or daily life is substantially affected, discuss pain control and an exercise plan and monitor the course rather than only waiting.

Q. Which shoulder-pain patients may consider Chuna or SART at Bodyall?

Consider an assessment when dressing, hair washing or reaching behind your back is difficult and you also want neck and upper-back restriction assessed. Bodyall compares active and passive shoulder movement and strength, using ultrasound as an adjunct when needed. Shoulder-joint Chuna, myofascial Chuna or MET is selected; SART is added when neck or thoracic correction is suitable. Acupotomy has a separate assessment of need and invasive risks, and the same daily function is reassessed.

Q. What does the acupotomy research tell us?

A 2022 randomised controlled study assigned 63 participants to hydrodilatation plus acupotomy (33) or hydrodilatation alone (30). At three months, active range of motion and Constant–Murley shoulder-function scores were higher in the combined-treatment group, with differences in several ultrasound measures. Because both groups received hydrodilatation, the study does not isolate the effect of acupotomy alone.

5. What Bodyall checks before and after care

At Bodyall Korean Medicine Clinic in Ingye-dong, Suwon, Chief Director Donghae Lee discusses treatment direction using your symptoms, history and movement findings. SART stands for Spine Alignment Restoration Therapy. Palpation, active and passive movement and posture guide the treatment area, direction and intensity, followed by active-movement reassessment and daily-activity education.

  • Explain when pain began, trauma or surgery, night waking and the most difficult activity.
  • Disclose diabetes, thyroid disease, osteoporosis, allergies, medicines and recent injections or procedures. Do not stop anticoagulants or antiplatelet medicines without the prescribing clinician.
  • Bring previous imaging, reports and treatment history when available. The need for further tests or medical referral is also considered.
  • After care, compare the same outcomes—dressing, raising the arm and sleep—not pain alone. New weakness, sensory change or worsening symptoms prompts reassessment of the plan.
