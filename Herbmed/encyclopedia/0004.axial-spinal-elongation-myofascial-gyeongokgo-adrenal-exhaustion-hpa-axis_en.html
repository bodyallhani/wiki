Persistent unrefreshing fatigue in Suwon: evaluation, Gyeongok-go suitability and musculoskeletal review

💡 Q. How does Bodyall assess fatigue when back or neck stiffness is also present?

At Bodyall, persistent unrefreshing fatigue that affects concentration or daily function is assessed by first reviewing its duration, sleep, meals, medicines and supplements, post-infection course and prior tests. We then consider whether Gyeongok-go or tailored herbal care fits, while movement-dependent neck, back or low-back symptoms receive their own musculoskeletal assessment.

Signs that need other medical assessment first

Unexplained weight loss, persistent fever or night sweats, chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, bleeding, new neurologic symptoms or rapidly worsening fatigue require appropriate medical evaluation before herbal or corrective care.

1. Assessing fatigue and accompanying pain together, but separately

The key is to avoid forcing fatigue into one cause or the label “adrenal fatigue,” and to make separate suitability decisions for herbal and musculoskeletal care.

Check

Review sleep quality and snoring, mood, menstruation or bleeding, digestion, activity, medicines, caffeine and alcohol, post-infection course, and prior test results.

Separate

Distinguish fatigue itself from neck, back or low-back discomfort that changes with movement. When indicated, discuss evaluation for anemia, thyroid disease, infection, sleep disorders or other causes.

Select

Use constitution, digestion and medication history to judge whether Gyeongok-go or tailored herbal care is appropriate. Only when musculoskeletal findings warrant it do we explain the separate roles of ultrasound, MET, Chuna and SART.

Reassess

Track fatigue severity, restoration after sleep and daily activity using the same measures. After musculoskeletal care, repeat the same movement and compare pain and range of motion.

Experience with spinal discomfort accompanying fatigue

Bodyall provides MET and ultrasound and had treated a cumulative 40,000 correction-care patients through December 2025 (clinic count). This experience helps us decide whether accompanying neck, back or low-back discomfort calls for musculoskeletal care and, when treated, reassess pain and range of motion with the same movement.

2. Roles of Gyeongok-go, tailored herbs, Chuna and SART

Gyeongok-go or tailored herbs

Suitability is based on the fatigue pattern, sleep, digestion, meals, medicines and constitution. One product is not prescribed uniformly for every cause of fatigue.

Chuna and MET

Options for joint and myofascial function when neck or back symptoms are reproducible during sitting, bending or rotation.

SART

Bodyall’s correction protocol for selected spinal and pelvic segment motion after confirming correction is appropriate. It is not presented as a direct treatment for fatigue or the HPA axis.

Referral and testing

Red flags, suspected systemic disease or inadequate response change the plan and may require further evaluation or referral.

3. What the evidence can support

Systematic review of manual therapy and ANS markers

A 2019 systematic review and meta-analysis of joint manipulative techniques reported that some mobilizations may produce immediate, short-term changes in skin sympathetic activity markers. It did not study chronic fatigue, HPA-axis recovery, spinal microcirculation, Gyeongok-go or SART, so it cannot show that spinal alignment causes fatigue or that correction normalizes adrenal function. (DOI).

Long-COVID Gyeongok-go pilot-study protocol

The 2025 PLOS ONE paper is a protocol for a double-blind, multicenter pilot study of Gyeongok-go and other herbal medicines in long-COVID fatigue. It is not a results paper and does not establish ordinary burnout treatment, individualized Bodyall outcomes, or superiority of combining herbs with Chuna or SART. (DOI).

Primary-care approach to persistent fatigue

Primary-care fatigue reviews recommend first identifying common causes, red flags, sleep and mental-health factors, medicines and possible systemic disease. Bodyall applies that separation before linking an appropriate patient to herbal or musculoskeletal care. (Evidence and limits).

4. Frequently asked questions

Should I take Gyeongok-go immediately if I think I have “adrenal fatigue”?

Fatigue has many possible causes. Duration, sleep, medicines, underlying conditions and red flags should be reviewed first. Gyeongok-go is selected only when the clinical assessment supports its suitability.

Must Gyeongok-go and SART be used together?

No. Herbal and musculoskeletal care require separate suitability decisions. The cited studies did not test superiority of the combination.

What should I prepare before the first visit?

Bring the onset of fatigue, sleep duration and restoration, weight change, menstruation or bleeding, medicines and supplements, caffeine and alcohol use, and prior test results. Note movements that reproduce spinal discomfort.

How is progress reassessed?

Track fatigue intensity, restoration after sleep and activity tolerance using the same criteria. If musculoskeletal care is performed, repeat the same movement and compare pain and range; change the plan when response is insufficient.

An assessment that does not force fatigue and spinal discomfort into one cause

If you want sleep, medicines, underlying conditions and herbal suitability checked first, with movement-dependent spinal symptoms assessed separately, include the onset of both problems and the function most affected when booking.
