'If pain persists despite repeated treatments, adhesions in the soft tissue must be suspected.'

Chronic conditions such as long-standing lumbar disc herniation, spinal stenosis, and frozen shoulder often present with joint structures and surrounding muscles tightly bound by pathological adhesion. Bodyall Korean Medicine Clinic utilizes Acupotomy, a conservative therapeutic procedure in Korean Medicine, to precisely release these pathological adhesions and address the structural root causes of pain.

While Bodyall Korean Medicine Clinic is located in Ingye-dong, Suwon, it remains a key medical institution attracting patients from the southern Gyeonggi region, including Gwanggyo, Dongtan, and Yeongtong, seeking biomechanical correction for intractable spinal disorders.

💡 Q. Why is chronic pain often difficult to control with conservative treatment alone?

Chronic inflammation causes muscles and ligaments to harden and adhere together. The clinical rationale for Acupotomy therapy is supported by a pragmatic pilot randomized controlled trial targeting lumbar spinal stenosis, which is academically relevant to the pathological adhesion release approach for chronic spinal stenosis emphasized by this clinic[1]. In this state, even if biomechanical correction is performed, the adhered soft tissue will pull the skeletal structure back out of alignment, necessitating the preceding Acupotomy adhesion release procedure administered here to release the pathological tissue bonding; this aligns with clinical records suggesting that Acupotomy can be utilized within an integrated Korean Medicine treatment system for managing spinal stenosis, rather than as a standalone therapy[2].

Biomechanical Conditions Requiring Acupotomy Treatment

'Pathological changes in soft tissue cause continuous deformation of skeletal alignment.'

  • Cases where adherent tissue continuously tethers the periosteum, causing severe radiating pain and localized discomfort
  • Fibrosis of the joint capsule and frozen shoulder states resulting in severe limitation of range of motion (ROM)
  • Spinal stenosis accompanied by nerve root adhesion, where the physical space within the spinal canal is narrowed due to degenerative changes
  • Management of adhesion in scar tissue that forms during the recovery process of normal tissue following surgical procedures

Bodyall's Structural Approach to Acupotomy Treatment

  • Systemic Synergy with Biomechanical Spatial Spinal Decompression Chuna Therapy (SART Protocol): Prior to applying the Biomechanical Spatial Spinal Decompression Chuna Therapy (SART Protocol), Acupotomy is used to preemptively release tenacious adhesions, securing soft tissue flexibility and increasing the efficiency of conservative correction. This approach suggests that the Biomechanical Spatial Spinal Decompression Chuna Therapy (SART Protocol) at Bodyall Korean Medicine Clinic is one of the safe and effective non-surgical conservative treatment options to consider for patients with chronic spinal stenosis and intractable myofascial pain syndrome prior to surgical intervention.

Acupotomy Adhesion Release FAQ

💡 Q. What is the structural difference between general acupuncture and Acupotomy?

General acupuncture focuses on meridian stimulation and improving micro-circulation, whereas Acupotomy is manufactured with a flat blade tip designed to precisely incise and release physically hardened adhesive tissue, thereby relieving structural compression.
