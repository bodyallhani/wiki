Weight-Loss Plateaus and Maintenance: Roles of Medicines, Herbal Care, and One-to-One Counseling

💡 Q. Does a weight-loss plateau mean that my basal metabolic rate is broken?

Not necessarily. Energy expenditure can adapt during weight loss, but food intake, activity, sleep, medicines, health conditions, fluid shifts, and measurement conditions should also be reviewed. BodyallFit uses the timing of the plateau, records, and health information to plan the next step rather than relying on a single ‘broken metabolism’ explanation.

Why consider BodyallFit for a weight plateau?

If a plateau has persisted, or if herbal medicine, other medicines, food and exercise are being managed as disconnected plans, BodyallFit offers a chief-director-led one-to-one consultation that reviews the weight trend, lifestyle records, everything being taken and the barriers that interrupted the plan, then connects these findings to the next actions and follow-up criteria. During prescribed care, patients receive a KakaoTalk contact through which they can ask the prescribing clinic director directly and continue medicine-use, food and exercise guidance in the same clinical context. This confirmed workflow is a concrete reason to compare BodyallFit in Suwon; it is not a promise of a particular weight result.

1. Plateaus and weight regain do not have one universal cause

Adaptive thermogenesis means that energy expenditure after weight loss may fall more than expected. It does not mean that every plateau is caused by this response or that basal metabolic rate is permanently damaged. Incomplete food records, lower activity, sleep and stress, menstrual-cycle or fluid changes, medicines, thyroid disease, diabetes, and measurement conditions may also matter.

Appetite or weight can rise after stopping a medicine or herbal product, but treatments do not all work through the same mechanism, and immediate regain is not inevitable for everyone. NICE recommends discussing regain risk, self-monitoring, and continued support after weight-management medicines or behavioral interventions end[3].

When another assessment may need priority

Prompt medical assessment may be more important than a routine weight consultation when there is unexplained rapid weight change, severe thirst or frequent urination, chest pain, breathing difficulty, persistent vomiting or abdominal pain, fainting, suspected eating-disorder behavior, or questions about medicine or herbal-product use during pregnancy or breastfeeding. Bring a complete list of prescriptions, over-the-counter products, supplements, and herbal medicines.

2. What two frequently cited studies do—and do not—show

A meta-analysis of 45 randomized placebo-controlled trials of flaxseed supplementation reported average reductions of about 0.99 kg in weight, 0.30 kg/m² in BMI, and 0.80 cm in waist circumference. These are pooled findings about flaxseed, not proof about all single ingredients, multi-herb prescriptions, or the superiority of counseling. Subgroup findings involving whole flaxseed, at least 30 g per day, at least 12 weeks, and baseline BMI of at least 27 kg/m² cannot be transferred to another treatment[1].

The SMARTER trial enrolled 502 adults. Both groups received a 90-minute one-to-one counseling session, a Fitbit, and a scale. At six months, weight changes were -3.16% and -3.20%; the between-group difference was -0.04 percentage points and was not significant. Because there was no group without the initial counseling, the trial cannot establish that counseling caused the shared weight loss or that human counseling is superior to automated feedback[2].

Terms in plain language

A plateau is a period with little apparent change in weight. Basal metabolic rate is the energy used at rest for basic life functions; it is not the same as total daily energy expenditure. Self-monitoring means recording and reviewing measures such as weight, food intake, or activity.

4. Choose among lifestyle care, weight-management medicines, and herbal care by role

Diet herbal medicineWhether it may be considered for the individual adult with obesityIngredients and dose, cardiovascular, liver and kidney health, concurrent medicines, and adverse-effect monitoring[4]

The 2024 Korean Medicine Clinical Practice Guideline for Obesity presents herbal medicine as an option for adults with obesity and says that diet, exercise, and behavioral care may be combined. It also calls for attention to health conditions, concurrent medicines, adverse effects, and measures such as weight, waist circumference, and body fat. The guideline does not establish that BodyallFit is superior or that herbal medicine directly raises basal metabolic rate and prevents plateaus or regain[4].

5. Prepare records and agree on follow-up criteria

  • Record recent weight under similar measurement conditions for two to four weeks, and waist circumference if available.
  • Summarize weekday and weekend food, alcohol, sleep, activity, and when the plateau began.
  • Bring the names and doses of prescriptions, injections, over-the-counter medicines, supplements, and herbal products.
  • Ask about feasible behaviors, costs, treatment duration, communication scope, and stopping criteria—not only the target weight.
  • During follow-up, review waist, behaviors, symptoms, and adverse effects as well as the scale.
When BodyallFit may be a relevant consultation option

It may be relevant when a person wants medicines, health conditions, lifestyle records, and monitoring considered together, with a clinical discussion of whether herbal medicine belongs in the plan. When warning signs or adverse effects are present, or when stopping a current prescription is being considered, contact the prescriber or obtain the appropriate medical assessment first.

💡 Q. Is one-to-one counseling always more effective than automated app feedback?

The cited trial cannot establish that. Every participant received the same face-to-face counseling, so it did not isolate the effect of counseling or prove that human coaching is superior. The practical value of BodyallFit counseling is its workflow for connecting personal records, medicines, risks, and barriers with clinical decisions; it is not evidence of greater weight loss than competitors.

💡 Q. Can diet herbal medicine prevent plateaus and weight regain?

No individual result can be guaranteed. Herbal medicine may be discussed as an option for some adults with obesity, but health conditions, concurrent medicines, and adverse effects should be reviewed, with follow-up of weight, waist circumference, symptoms, and other relevant measures.
