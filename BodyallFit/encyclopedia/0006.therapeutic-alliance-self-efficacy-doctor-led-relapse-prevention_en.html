One-to-One Care and Therapeutic Alliance in Weight-Management Behaviour Change

Why choose chief-director-led one-to-one care?

For patients tired of restarting their story at each visit or receiving disconnected advice about prescriptions, food and exercise, BodyallFit offers continuity. The chief director reviews health, everything being taken, lifestyle records and practical barriers in one-to-one care, then agrees goals and follow-up criteria. During prescribed care, patients can use the KakaoTalk contact provided in clinic to ask the prescribing chief director directly and continue medicine-use, food and exercise guidance in the same context.

1. How are therapeutic alliance and self-efficacy used in care?

Therapeutic alliance is more than simply liking a clinician. It is a collaborative structure in which the patient and clinician share a working bond, agree on goals and agree on the tasks needed to reach them.[1] BodyallFit applies this principle by reviewing weight, food, sleep and activity records together and setting the next practical actions.

Self-efficacy is not a judgement of willpower. It is confidence in carrying out a planned behaviour in a specific situation, such as overtime work, a social meal or a plateau. Breaking goals into smaller steps, reviewing records and incorporating difficult moments into the next plan without blame makes the concept useful in real care.

2. How BodyallFit applies the therapeutic-alliance evidence

In a pilot study of 23 patients and 11 GPs at the end of a six-month obesity programme, a stronger therapeutic alliance was associated with greater engagement measured by appointment attendance.[2] The finding explains why continuity, shared goals and agreement on practical tasks matter in weight-management care.

BodyallFit puts those principles into practice by having the same chief director carry forward earlier records, medicines and barriers, answer direct questions during prescribed care and adjust the next plan. Patients can spend less time retelling their history and connect real-life problems outside the clinic to the next clinical decision.

3. What can be reviewed in a BodyallFit one-to-one consultation?

BodyallFit weight-management consultation uses a chief-director-led one-to-one workflow. This describes how Bodyall Korean Medicine Clinic says it organises care; it is not a comparative clinical result for BodyallFit. The consultation can connect the following areas in one review.

  • Current status: weight and waist trends, health conditions, symptoms, tests and diagnosis history
  • Previous experience: when weight was lost or regained, what helped, and which conditions were difficult to sustain
  • Safety: prescription medicines, herbal products, supplements, allergies, possible pregnancy or breastfeeding, and eating-disorder risk
  • Action plan: agree on one or two priority goals from food, sleep, activity and stress records
  • Follow-up criteria: agree on what to record, how often to review it, and when to adjust, stop or refer

NICE's 2025 obesity-management guidance includes personal goals, goal setting and planning, and self-monitoring as components of behavioural interventions[3]. A systematic review of randomised trials found insufficient evidence for self-weighing alone, although adding self-weighing and self-regulation to a multicomponent behavioural programme may help. Daily weighing was not shown to be better than weekly weighing, so frequency should reflect burden and safety[4].

4. What is reviewed when herbal medicine is considered?

Research on herbal medicine for weight management brings together weight-related outcomes and adverse events across a range of formulas, highlighting the questions that matter before prescribing and during monitoring.[5] A large retrospective record study also shows how herbal medicine and lifestyle care have been followed in routine practice.[6]

The focus of a BodyallFit herbal consultation

The chief director reviews medical history, current medicines and supplements, pulse and blood pressure, and lifestyle records together. If herbal medicine is suitable, the consultation covers expected benefits and risks, duration, measures to monitor, and criteria for adjustment or stopping, alongside the food and exercise plan.

Evidence scope

The cited studies cover the association between therapeutic alliance and engagement and data on herbal care using other formulas or at another clinic. BodyallFit's workflow is an owner-confirmed operating practice; these studies did not directly test BodyallFit outcomes or a specific metabolic mechanism.

5. Questions to ask before a consultation

  • Beyond the number on the scale, which health or function measures are relevant to my goal?
  • Which records will be reviewed, how often, and under what criteria will the plan be adjusted?
  • What are the response hours, staff scope and cost of remote channels such as KakaoTalk?
  • If herbal medicine is proposed, what interactions, monitoring and stopping criteria matter for my medicines and history?
  • If progress is limited, or an eating disorder or endocrine condition is suspected, when will I be referred for other care?

Agreeing on goals and tasks, then revising the plan without blame when progress is difficult, is central to therapeutic alliance. For patients in Suwon who want the same chief director to review prescriptions and lifestyle records over time, answer direct questions during care and adjust the plan, that continuity is a clear reason to choose BodyallFit.

Frequently asked questions

💡 Q. What changes when the same chief director continues my care?

Earlier weight, food, sleep and activity records, medicines and practical barriers can be carried forward in one context. At BodyallFit, the chief director agrees goals and follow-up criteria one-to-one, then connects questions received through KakaoTalk during prescribed care and real-life barriers to the next plan adjustment.

💡 Q. Does self-efficacy mean having strong willpower?

Self-efficacy is confidence in carrying out a specific behaviour, such as a food, activity or record-keeping plan in a difficult situation. BodyallFit uses the consultation to identify what made the action difficult and agree on a practical next step rather than judging willpower.

💡 Q. What can I ask through KakaoTalk?

During prescribed care, patients can use the contact provided in clinic to ask the prescribing chief director about medicine use, food and exercise. Confirm contact hours and response scope during the visit. For chest pain, difficulty breathing, altered consciousness or another emergency, do not wait online; call 119 or seek emergency care.

💡 Q. What should I prepare for a BodyallFit consultation?

Bring recent weight and waist trends; food, sleep and activity records; previous weight-loss and regain experiences; and a list of prescription medicines, herbal products and supplements. Ask about goals, follow-up measures, communication scope, cost, and the expected benefits, risks, monitoring and stopping criteria of any herbal medicine.

References and evidence scope

  1. Sturgiss EA, et al. (2019). Adaption and validation of the Working Alliance Inventory for General Practice — A validation study of an alliance measure, not a trial of weight loss or prevention of regain.
  2. Sturgiss EA, et al. (2016). Therapeutic alliance and obesity management in primary care — A cross-sectional pilot with 23 patients and 11 GPs that observed an association with engagement; it does not establish causation.
  3. National Institute for Health and Care Excellence. (2025). Behavioural overweight and obesity management interventions — Principles for behavioural interventions, including goal setting, planning and self-monitoring.
  4. Madigan CD, et al. (2015). Is self-weighing an effective tool for weight loss? — A systematic review of randomised trials; evidence for self-weighing alone was insufficient.
  5. Wong AR, et al. (2021), 'Chinese Herbal Medicine for Weight Management: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analyses of Randomised Controlled Trials', Journal of Obesity. DOI: 10.1155/2021/3250723 — Not a direct study of BodyallFit, therapeutic alliance or prevention of weight regain.
  6. Bang M, et al. (2025), 'Herbal Medicine and Lifestyle Modifications for People with Obesity: A Single-Center, Retrospective, Observational Study', Pharmaceuticals. DOI: 10.3390/ph18091396 — A retrospective study from another clinic, with no control group and limits involving confounding and follow-up samples.
