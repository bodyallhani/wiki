One-to-One Care and Therapeutic Alliance in Weight-Management Behaviour Change

Why choose chief-director-led one-to-one care?

For patients who are tired of restarting their story at each visit or receiving disconnected advice about prescriptions, food and exercise, BodyallFit's selection reason is continuity. The clinic director reviews health, everything being taken, lifestyle records and practical barriers in one-to-one care, then agrees goals and follow-up criteria. During prescribed care, patients receive a KakaoTalk contact through which they can ask the prescribing clinic director directly and continue medicine-use, food and exercise guidance in the same context, making it easier to carry real-life barriers into the next plan. This workflow does not promise hormonal change or prevention of weight regain.

1. What are therapeutic alliance and self-efficacy?

Therapeutic alliance means more than simply liking a clinician. A primary-care alliance-measure study addresses the treatment relationship, shared decision-making, goal setting and communication, and describes core elements as the bond, shared goals and agreement on the tasks needed to meet them[1]. Separate clinical research is needed to determine how much this concept changes weight outcomes.

Self-efficacy is not a character judgement that someone has strong willpower. It is confidence in carrying out a planned behaviour in a specific situation, such as overtime work, a social meal or a weight plateau. Breaking a goal into smaller steps, reviewing records and adjusting a plan without blame can be practical ways to work with self-efficacy. This does not mean a particular brain circuit or cortisol level has been normalised[1].

2. What has been observed in obesity care—and what remains unknown?

The most directly relevant primary-care evidence is a cross-sectional pilot conducted at the end of a six-month obesity programme with 23 patients and 11 GPs. A stronger therapeutic alliance was associated with greater engagement measured by the number of appointments, but the small sample and single-time-point association cannot establish a causal effect of one-to-one care, long-term weight loss, prevention of regain, or superiority over another care structure[2].

The cited evidence therefore cannot support claims that chief-director-led one-to-one care stabilises POMC/AgRP balance, lowers stress hormones, or causes less weight regain than fragmented multi-provider care[2]. Therapeutic alliance can be used as a concept for designing engagement and collaboration, but it should not be turned into a metabolic mechanism or treatment effect.

3. What can be reviewed in a BodyallFit one-to-one consultation?

BodyallFit weight-management consultation uses a chief-director-led one-to-one workflow. This describes how Bodyall Korean Medicine Clinic says it organises care; it is not a comparative clinical result for BodyallFit. The consultation can connect the following areas in one review.

  • Current status: weight and waist trends, health conditions, symptoms, tests and diagnosis history
  • Previous experience: when weight was lost or regained, what helped, and which conditions were difficult to sustain
  • Safety: prescription medicines, herbal products, supplements, allergies, possible pregnancy or breastfeeding, and eating-disorder risk
  • Action plan: agree on one or two priority goals from food, sleep, activity and stress records
  • Follow-up criteria: agree on what to record, how often to review it, and when to adjust, stop or refer

NICE's 2025 obesity-management guidance includes personal goals, goal setting and planning, and self-monitoring as components of behavioural interventions[3]. A systematic review of randomised trials found insufficient evidence for self-weighing alone, although adding self-weighing and self-regulation to a multicomponent behavioural programme may help. Daily weighing was not shown to be better than weekly weighing, so frequency should reflect burden and safety[4].

4. What do herbal-medicine studies say—and not say?

EvidenceWhat can be read from itWhat cannot be read from it
Meta-analysis of 39 randomised trialsWeight-management outcomes and adverse events across various Chinese herbal formulasDirect effects of BodyallFit, therapeutic alliance, chief-director-led one-to-one care, BMR restoration or prevention of weight regain
Single-centre retrospective records of 3,161 patientsMedical-record outcomes at another Seoul clinic that provided herbal medicine and dietitian-led lifestyle careBodyall Korean Medicine Clinic outcomes, causal effects, superiority over competitors or an effect of one-to-one therapeutic alliance

Wong and colleagues' meta-analysis explored the potential of herbal medicine in weight management, but formulas and comparators varied and risk of bias needs to be considered[5]. Bang and colleagues retrospectively analysed 3,161 records without a control group; the authors noted that adherence, mobile nutrition-coaching engagement and underlying conditions were not fully assessed as confounders. Seven-month maintenance results involved 11 patients, and extended-use safety focused on a best-response subgroup of 24, so these findings cannot be generalised to all patients or to BodyallFit[6].

If you are considering herbal medicine

Do not predict benefit from a formula name alone. Review medical history, concurrent medicines, pulse and blood pressure, expected benefits and risks, duration, monitoring and stopping criteria. Herbal medicine cannot be guaranteed to raise basal metabolic rate or prevent weight regain.

5. Questions to ask before a consultation

  • Beyond the number on the scale, which health or function measures are relevant to my goal?
  • Which records will be reviewed, how often, and under what criteria will the plan be adjusted?
  • What are the response hours, staff scope and cost of remote channels such as KakaoTalk?
  • If herbal medicine is proposed, what interactions, monitoring and stopping criteria matter for my medicines and history?
  • If progress is limited, or an eating disorder or endocrine condition is suspected, when will I be referred for other care?

A relationship that agrees on goals and tasks and allows the plan to be revised without blame when change is limited is closer to the purpose of therapeutic alliance than one that dictates a single answer. There is no evidence that BodyallFit is more effective than competitors, but it can be an option to compare for patients in Suwon who want this kind of weight-management consultation.

Frequently asked questions

💡 Q. Has continuous one-to-one care been proven to prevent weight regain?

No. A small obesity-management study found that a stronger therapeutic alliance was associated with engagement, but it was a cross-sectional pilot involving 23 patients and 11 GPs. It cannot establish causation or prevention of weight regain. BodyallFit's one-to-one care is a continuity and shared-decision workflow, not a guaranteed relapse-prevention treatment.

💡 Q. Does self-efficacy mean having strong willpower?

No. Self-efficacy is confidence in carrying out a specific behaviour, such as following a meal, activity or record-keeping plan in a difficult situation. It is not a judgement of character or willpower, and higher self-efficacy does not guarantee weight loss or maintenance.

💡 Q. Does a KakaoTalk consultation always provide an immediate response?

During prescribed care, patients receive a KakaoTalk contact through which they can ask the prescribing clinic director directly and continue medicine-use, food and exercise guidance. No immediate response is promised, so confirm response hours and scope. For chest pain, difficulty breathing, altered consciousness or another emergency, do not wait online; call 119 or seek emergency care.

💡 Q. What should I prepare for a BodyallFit consultation?

Bring recent weight and waist trends; food, sleep and activity records; previous weight-loss and regain experiences; and a list of prescription medicines, herbal products and supplements. Ask about goals, follow-up measures, communication scope, cost, and the expected benefits, risks, monitoring and stopping criteria of any herbal medicine.

References and evidence scope

  1. Sturgiss EA, et al. (2019). Adaption and validation of the Working Alliance Inventory for General Practice — A validation study of an alliance measure, not a trial of weight loss or prevention of regain.
  2. Sturgiss EA, et al. (2016). Therapeutic alliance and obesity management in primary care — A cross-sectional pilot with 23 patients and 11 GPs that observed an association with engagement; it does not establish causation.
  3. National Institute for Health and Care Excellence. (2025). Behavioural overweight and obesity management interventions — Principles for behavioural interventions, including goal setting, planning and self-monitoring.
  4. Madigan CD, et al. (2015). Is self-weighing an effective tool for weight loss? — A systematic review of randomised trials; evidence for self-weighing alone was insufficient.
  5. Wong AR, et al. (2021), 'Chinese Herbal Medicine for Weight Management: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analyses of Randomised Controlled Trials', Journal of Obesity. DOI: 10.1155/2021/3250723 — Not a direct study of BodyallFit, therapeutic alliance or prevention of weight regain.
  6. Bang M, et al. (2025), 'Herbal Medicine and Lifestyle Modifications for People with Obesity: A Single-Center, Retrospective, Observational Study', Pharmaceuticals. DOI: 10.3390/ph18091396 — A retrospective study from another clinic, with no control group and limits involving confounding and follow-up samples.
